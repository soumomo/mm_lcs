Section 4.1 • System Representation Theory

Fundamentals of Block Diagrams & The Canonical Closed-Loop System

Mathematical foundations, unilateral signal propagation, the non-loading assumption, and algebraic derivation of all canonical transfer ratios.

The Philosophy of Block Diagrams in Control Systems Engineering

In linear control theory, a Block Diagram is a graphical representation of the mathematical cause-and-effect relationships governing a physical system. Each element or subsystem is abstracted as a directional operational block characterized entirely by its Laplace-domain Transfer Function:

$$G(s) = \frac{\mathcal{L}\{\text{output}(t)\}}{\mathcal{L}\{\text{input}(t)\}}\Bigg|_{\text{zero initial conditions}} = \frac{C(s)}{R(s)}$$
1. Unidirectional Signal Flow

Information moves strictly along the directed arrows from input to output. The output signal $C(s) = G(s) R(s)$ cannot exert any reverse physical loading or back-reaction on the input $R(s)$ through the block itself.

2. The Non-Loading Assumption

Cascading blocks assumes the subsequent stage presents infinite input impedance (or zero load current) to the preceding stage. If loading occurs (e.g. cascaded passive $RC$ filters), the blocks must be derived from coupled equations rather than individual uncoupled transfers.

The Three Primitive Elements of Every Block Diagram

Fig. 1 (MM Lecture Notes, p. 1)
Transfer Function Block: Linear operator performing continuous frequency multiplication: $Y(s) = G(s)X(s)$. Internal physical state variables are compressed into the input-output ratio.
Summing Point (Comparator): Performs instantaneous algebraic addition or subtraction. The sign ($+$ or −) must be explicitly specified near the arrow entering the circle.
Take-off Point: Distributes identical, undisturbed copies of the signal variable to multiple downstream branches concurrently without power drain or attenuation.

The Canonical Closed-Loop Feedback Control System

Rigorous algebraic derivation of Closed-Loop Transfer Function, Actuating Error Ratio, and Feedback Ratio.

Core Standard Model
Ratio 1: Control Ratio T(s)

Closed-Loop Transfer Function

From the forward plant and summer equations:

C(s) = G(s) E(s)   …(1)
E(s) = R(s) − B(s)
= R(s) − H(s)C(s) …(2)

Substitute (2) into (1):

$$C(s) = G(s)[R(s) \mp H(s)C(s)]$$ $$C(s)[1 \pm G(s)H(s)] = G(s)R(s)$$
$$\frac{C(s)}{R(s)} = T(s) = \frac{G(s)}{1 \pm G(s)H(s)}$$
$+$ for negative feedback; − for positive feedback.
Ratio 2: Error Ratio E(s)/R(s)

Actuating Error Transfer Function

Substitute $C(s) = G(s)E(s)$ directly into error equation:

E(s) = R(s) − H(s)[G(s)E(s)]
E(s) [1 + G(s)H(s)] = R(s)

Solving directly for $E(s)/R(s)$:

$$\frac{E(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{1}{1 \pm G(s)H(s)}$$

Control Implication: High loop gain $|G(j\omega)H(j\omega)| \gg 1$ attenuates steady-state tracking error to near zero ($E(s) \to 0$).

Ratio 3 & Char Eqn B(s)/R(s)

Primary Feedback Ratio

Since $B(s) = H(s)C(s)$, multiplying $T(s)$ by $H(s)$ gives:

$$\frac{B(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{G(s)H(s)}{1 \pm G(s)H(s)}$$
The Characteristic Equation
$$1 \pm G(s)H(s) = 0$$

The roots of this equation are the closed-loop poles which exclusively govern stability, damping factor $\zeta$, and natural frequency $\omega_n$.

JU Class Test 2024 / Semester Exam Focus Negative (Degenerative) vs. Positive (Regenerative) Feedback
Negative Feedback ($E = R - B$):
  • Denominator is $1 + G(s)H(s)$.
  • Reduces overall system gain by factor $1/(1+GH)$, trading gain for accuracy.
  • Extends system bandwidth and suppresses disturbances entering the plant.
  • Significantly desensitizes system to parameter drifts and component aging ($S^T_G = 1/(1+GH)$).
Positive Feedback ($E = R + B$):
  • Denominator is $1 - G(s)H(s)$.
  • If loop gain $G(s)H(s) \to 1$, denominator goes to zero and closed-loop gain approaches $\infty$.
  • Drives closed-loop poles into the Right-Half $s$-plane, triggering exponential instability.
  • Engineering Applications: Sinusoidal Barkhausen oscillators (Wien-bridge, Colpitts where $A\beta = 1 \angle 0^\circ$), multivibrators, Schmitt triggers with hysteresis.
Section 4.2 • Systematic Topological Equivalence

The 10 Master Block Diagram Reduction Rules

Exhaustive Before & After vector SVG diagrams, algebraic invariance proofs, the Golden Duality Mnemonic, and common exam pitfalls.

The Golden Duality Mnemonic • Never Confuse Operations in Exams
SUMMING POINT (Σ) MOVEMENTS
• Moving ALONG signal arrow (after block): Introduce G on branch.
• Moving AGAINST signal arrow (ahead of block): Introduce 1/G on branch.
TAKE-OFF POINT (•) MOVEMENTS (EXACT OPPOSITE!)
• Moving ALONG signal arrow (after block): Introduce 1/G on branch.
• Moving AGAINST signal arrow (ahead of block): Introduce G on branch.
1

Blocks in Cascade (Series)

G1 × G2

Algebraic Proof: Intermediate signal $W = G_1 X$. Final output $Y = G_2 W = G_2(G_1 X) = (G_1 G_2) X$.

Constraint: Valid strictly when there are no intermediate pick-off points or summers between $G_1$ and $G_2$, and no loading occurs.

2

Blocks in Parallel (Feedforward)

G1 ± G2

Algebraic Proof: $Y = G_1 X \pm G_2 X = (G_1 \pm G_2) X$.

Rule-of-Thumb: Parallel branches originate from a common pick-off point and terminate at a common summing junction, pointing in the same direction.

3

Eliminating a Minor Feedback Loop

G / (1 ∓ GH)

Algebraic Proof: $Y = G(X \mp HY) \implies Y(1 \pm GH) = GX \implies \frac{Y}{X} = \frac{G}{1 \pm GH}$.

Sign Rule: − feedback at summer yields $+$ in denominator; $+$ feedback yields − in denominator.

4

Moving a Summer Ahead of a Block

Introduce 1/G

Algebraic Proof: Original $Y = X_1 G \pm X_2$. Transformed $Y = G(X_1 \pm \frac{X_2}{G}) = X_1 G \pm X_2$. Invariant!

Mnemonic: Moving summer against signal direction divides incoming branch by $G$ (multiply by $1/G$).

5

Moving a Summer After a Block

Introduce G

Algebraic Proof: Original $Y = G(X_1 \pm X_2) = X_1 G \pm X_2 G$. In transformed diagram, summer adds $X_1 G$ and $X_2 G$. Exact identity!

Mnemonic: Moving summer along signal direction multiplies incoming branch by $G$.

6

Moving a Take-off Point Ahead of a Block

Introduce G

Algebraic Proof: Original tapped signal is $XG$. If tapped before $G$, the signal available is only $X$; therefore, multiplying by $G$ restores the required $XG$.

Mnemonic: Moving pick-off against signal direction adds a block $G$ in the tapped path.

7

Moving a Take-off Point After a Block

Introduce 1/G

Algebraic Proof: Original tapped branch carried $X$. Tapping after $G$ gives $XG$. Multiplying by $\frac{1}{G}$ yields $(XG)\frac{1}{G} = X$.

Mnemonic: Moving pick-off along signal direction divides tapped branch by $G$ (multiply by $1/G$).

8

Interchanging Adjacent Summing Points

Commutativity

Algebraic Proof: Addition of linear signals is commutative and associative: $(X_1 \pm X_2) \pm X_3 = (X_1 \pm X_3) \pm X_2$.

Crucial Rule: Interchanging is permissible only if no pick-off point or intermediate block is located between the two summers!

9

Splitting & Combining Summing Points

Topological Expansion

Utility in Reduction: A complicated 3-input or 4-input summer can be split into two 2-input summers. This isolates inner loops and allows standard minor-loop reduction.

10

Moving Pick-off Across Summing Point

Cross-Junction Shift

Heuristic Warning: Moving a take-off point across a summing point requires duplicating the addition/subtraction in the tapped branch. This usually creates extra complexity; therefore, always prefer moving take-off points past blocks rather than across summing points!

Section 4.3 • Algorithmic Reduction & Physical Modeling

Systematic Reduction Procedure & The Interacting Liquid Tanks Case Study

The 5-step heuristic algorithm, coupled differential equations, block diagram synthesis, and step-by-step reduction of Prof. Madhubanti Maitra's classic two-tank hydraulic system.

The 5-Step Golden Heuristic for Complex Multi-Loop Reduction

When faced with heavily cross-coupled block diagrams in Jadavpur University semester examinations, apply this standardized 5-step priority sequence to prevent endless loops and eliminate cross-coupling cleanly:

STEP 1 Trivial
Cascade & Parallel

Immediately combine purely cascaded blocks ($G_1 G_2$) and purely parallel forward paths ($G_1 \pm G_2$).

STEP 2 Inner
Minor Feedback

Identify and eliminate all independent, uncoupled internal feedback loops using $G/(1 \pm GH)$.

STEP 3 KEY
Shift Across Blocks

Shift pick-off points downstream (to the right, past blocks) or summers upstream (to the left) to uncouple intersecting loops.

STEP 4 Secondary
Reduce New Loops

Repeat cascade and minor loop reductions on newly isolated sub-loops created after shifting junctions.

STEP 5 Final
Outer Canonical

Reduce the remaining single-loop canonical feedback system into the final consolidated transfer function $C(s)/R(s)$.

Physical Case Study: Two Interacting Liquid-Level Tanks

First-principles physical modeling, block diagram formulation, and 5-stage reduction of hydraulic cross-coupling.

JU Syllabus Benchmark

Consider two cascading tanks with capacitances $C_1, C_2$ and restrictor resistances $R_1, R_2$. Because outflow rate $Q_1$ depends simultaneously on head $H_1$ and back-pressure head $H_2$, Tank 2 interacts with Tank 1. The system cannot be solved by simply cascading independent transfer functions!

• Flow balance Tank 1: $\Delta Q(s) - \Delta Q_1(s) = s C_1 \Delta H_1(s) \implies \Delta H_1(s) = \frac{1}{s C_1} [\Delta Q(s) - \Delta Q_1(s)]$
• Flow balance Tank 2: $\Delta Q_1(s) - \Delta Q_2(s) = s C_2 \Delta H_2(s) \implies \Delta H_2(s) = \frac{1}{s C_2} [\Delta Q_1(s) - \Delta Q_2(s)]$
• Interconnecting Pipe: $\Delta Q_1(s) = \frac{\Delta H_1(s) - \Delta H_2(s)}{R_1} = \frac{1}{R_1} [\Delta H_1(s) - \Delta H_2(s)]$
• Free Discharge Pipe: $\Delta Q_2(s) = \frac{\Delta H_2(s)}{R_2} = \frac{1}{R_2} \Delta H_2(s)$

Stage 0: Synthesized Interacting Block Diagram (Fig. 6g of MM Notes)

Cross-coupling at T1 and T2
Stages 1 & 2: Uncoupling Inner Loop
  • Shift Take-off Point $T_1$ after block $1/R_2$: By Rule 7, moving pick-off point $T_1$ after $1/R_2$ introduces a block $R_2$ into the tapped feedback branch returning to Summer 2.
  • Eliminate Tank 2 Minor Loop: The minor feedback loop with forward block $1/(sC_2)$ and unity feedback of $\Delta Q_2$ reduces to: $$\frac{1/(sC_2)}{1 + 1/(sC_2 \cdot R_2)} \times \frac{1}{R_2} = \frac{1}{R_2 C_2 s + 1}$$
Stages 3 & 4: Shifting T2 & Inner Loop
  • Shift Take-off Point $T_2$ to the right: Moving $T_2$ past the block $\frac{1}{R_2 C_2 s + 1}$ introduces the reciprocal factor $(R_2 C_2 s + 1)$ on the feedback path of $\Delta Q_1$ returning to Summer 1.
  • Reduce Encircled Minor Loop: Forward block $1/R_1$ with feedback gain $R_2$ in series with $\frac{1}{R_2 C_2 s + 1}$ simplifies to: $$\frac{1}{R_1 R_2 C_2 s + R_1 + R_2}$$
Stage 5: Final Reduced System Transfer Function Exact Analytical Form
$$\frac{\Delta Q_2(s)}{\Delta Q(s)} = \frac{1}{R_1 C_1 R_2 C_2 s^2 + (R_1 C_1 + R_2 C_2 + R_2 C_1) s + 1}$$
Interacting Tanks (Actual System):

Denominator includes the extra interaction term $\mathbf{+ R_2 C_1 s}$. The natural frequency $\omega_n$ decreases, damping ratio $\zeta$ increases, and transient response becomes more heavily damped and sluggish.

Non-Interacting Tanks (Decoupled Baseline):

If an isolating pump prevented back-pressure, the transfer function would simply be $\frac{1}{(R_1 C_1 s + 1)(R_2 C_2 s + 1)}$, completely lacking the $R_2 C_1 s$ cross-coupling coefficient.

Section 4.4 • Superposition & Robustness

Multi-Input Systems & Disturbance Rejection

Linear superposition analysis, reference tracking vs. load disturbance attenuation, controller gain synthesis, and MIMO matrix extensions.

The Principle of Superposition for Multi-Input Linear Systems

In real-world control applications (such as a speed-controlled DC motor subjected to sudden mechanical load torque, or an aircraft encountering atmospheric wind gusts), systems are constantly driven by both an intended Reference Command $R(s)$ and an unwanted Disturbance / Load Noise $D(s)$. Because the system is linear and time-invariant (LTI), the total output is the algebraic sum of the individual outputs evaluated with all other inputs set to zero:

$$C(s) = C_R(s)\Big|_{D(s)=0} + C_D(s)\Big|_{R(s)=0}$$

Superposition Analysis: Canonical Disturbance Topology

Fig. 3 (MM Notes, p. 4)
1. Reference Tracking Transfer Ratio D(s)=0
$$C_R(s) = \frac{G_1(s) G_2(s)}{1 + G_1(s) G_2(s) H(s)} R(s)$$

Both controller $G_1$ and plant $G_2$ reside on the direct forward path between reference input $R(s)$ and output $C_R(s)$.

2. Disturbance Transfer Ratio R(s)=0
$$C_D(s) = \frac{G_2(s)}{1 + G_1(s) G_2(s) H(s)} D(s)$$

Critical Observation: The forward path for disturbance $D(s)$ is only $G_2(s)$, while $G_1(s)$ is relegated to the feedback loop!

Consolidated Total Output by Superposition
$$C(s) = C_R(s) + C_D(s) = \frac{G_2(s)}{1 + G_1(s) G_2(s) H(s)} \Big[ G_1(s) R(s) + D(s) \Big]$$
The Classical Controller Design Trade-off: High Pre-Filter Gain

Examine the two transfer functions when the controller gain $|G_1(s)|$ is deliberately designed to be very large in the operational frequency band:

Tracking Transfer Function: $$\lim_{|G_1| \to \infty} \frac{C_R(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{G_1 G_2}{G_1 G_2 H} = \frac{1}{H(s)}$$ (If unity feedback $H=1$, then $C_R(s) \to R(s)$, perfect tracking!)
Disturbance Transfer Function: $$\lim_{|G_1| \to \infty} \frac{C_D(s)}{D(s)} = \frac{G_2}{G_1 G_2 H} = \frac{1}{G_1(s) H(s)} \to 0$$ (Disturbance is completely suppressed at output!)

Practical Rule: Place controller amplifier $G_1(s)$ before the point where disturbance enters to simultaneously maximize tracking sensitivity and minimize disturbance transmission.

Section 4.5 • Topological Graph Theory

Signal Flow Graphs (SFG) & Mason's Gain Formula

Formal definitions, topological graph algebra, the 2-minute Block-to-SFG conversion methodology, and Prof. MM's 5-loop non-touching benchmark.

Why Signal Flow Graphs? The Power of S.J. Mason's Discovery (1953)

While block diagram reduction is physically intuitive, simplifying heavily cross-coupled systems by moving summing points and pick-off points step-by-step is notoriously tedious, time-consuming, and prone to algebraic errors under exam pressure. In 1953, Samuel Jefferson Mason developed Signal Flow Graphs and formulated a universal gain theorem that completely eliminates step-by-step reductions:

$$T = \frac{C(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{1}{\Delta} \sum_{k=1}^N P_k \Delta_k$$

By simple topological inspection of the graph, one directly writes down the forward paths, feedback loops, and determinant cofactors in four lines of algebra!

Topological Vocabulary of Signal Flow Graphs

1. Node: A point representing a system variable or signal (e.g. $X_1, E, C$). Its value equals the sum of all incoming branch signals.
2. Branch & Transmittance: A directed line connecting two nodes. The signal is multiplied by the branch gain or transmittance $T_{ij}$ during transmission.
3. Input Node (Source): A node that possesses strictly outgoing branches only (e.g. Reference $R$). No signals enter a source node.
4. Output Node (Sink): A node that possesses strictly incoming branches only (e.g. $C$). If an output taps feedback, add a virtual branch with gain $1$ to create a true sink!
5. Forward Path ($P_k$): A path starting at an input node and terminating at an output node along which no node is traversed more than once.
6. Feedback Loop ($L_m$): A closed path originating and terminating at the same node along which no other node is crossed more than once.

Mathematical Anatomy of Mason's Gain Formula

$$T = \frac{1}{\Delta} \sum_{k=1}^N P_k \Delta_k = \frac{P_1 \Delta_1 + P_2 \Delta_2 + \dots + P_N \Delta_N}{\Delta}$$
1. The Graph Determinant ($\Delta$):
$$\Delta = 1 - \sum L_a + \sum L_b L_c - \sum L_d L_e L_f + \dots$$
  • $\sum L_a$ = Sum of loop gains of all individual loops.
  • $\sum L_b L_c$ = Sum of gain products of all possible pairs of non-touching loops (loops with zero shared nodes).
  • $\sum L_d L_e L_f$ = Sum of gain products of all triplets of mutually non-touching loops.
  • Alternating signs: $1 - (\text{singles}) + (\text{pairs}) - (\text{triplets}) + \dots$
2. Path Factors / Cofactors ($\Delta_k$):
$$\Delta_k = \Delta\Big|_{\text{loops touching path } k \text{ set to } 0}$$
  • $\Delta_k$ is the determinant of that sub-graph which does not touch the $k$-th forward path.
  • Mental Shortcut: Mentally erase path $k$ and all nodes it passes through; then compute $\Delta$ for whatever remains.
  • Standard Case: If the $k$-th forward path touches every loop in the system, then $\Delta_k = 1 - 0 = \mathbf{1}$.

Equations (10) Case Study

Consider the system described by the following set of simultaneous algebraic equations:

x2 = a12x1 + a32x3 + a42x4 + a52x5
x3 = a23x2
x4 = a34x3 + a44x4
x5 = a35x3 + a45x4
Forward Paths ($P_k$) & Cofactors ($\Delta_k$):
• Path 1: $x_1 \to x_2 \to x_3 \to x_4 \to x_5$
$P_1 = a_{12} a_{23} a_{34} a_{45}$
Touches all 5 loops $\implies \mathbf{\Delta_1 = 1}$
• Path 2: $x_1 \to x_2 \to x_3 \to x_5$
$P_2 = a_{12} a_{23} a_{35}$
Does NOT touch node $x_4$ (self-loop $a_{44}$) $\implies \mathbf{\Delta_2 = 1 - a_{44}}$
Feedback Loops & Non-Touching Pairs:
$L_1 = a_{23} a_{32}$   ($x_2 \to x_3 \to x_2$)
$L_2 = a_{23} a_{34} a_{42}$   ($x_2 \to x_3 \to x_4 \to x_2$)
$L_3 = a_{44}$   (self-loop at $x_4$)

$L_5 = a_{23} a_{35} a_{52}$   ($x_2 \to x_3 \to x_5 \to x_2$)
Non-Touching Pairs (2 pairs):
• $L_1$ and $L_3$: $P_{12} = a_{23} a_{32} a_{44}$
• $L_5$ and $L_3$: $P_{22} = a_{23} a_{35} a_{52} a_{44}$

Topic: SFG & Non-Touching Loops

University Examination Question:

“Construct the Signal Flow Graph for the system shown in Fig. P-2. Find out the closed loop transfer function of the system using Mason's Gain Formula.”

Step-by-Step Analytical Derivation:
1. Forward Paths ($P_k$):
• $P_1 = R \to \Sigma_1 \to \Sigma_2 \to G_1 \to G_2 \to \Sigma_3 \to G_3 \to C$
$P_1 = G_1 G_2 G_3$
• $P_2 = R \to \Sigma_1 \to \Sigma_2 \to G_1 \to G_4 \to \Sigma_3 \to G_3 \to C$
$P_2 = G_1 G_4 G_3$
2. Feedback Loops ($L_i$):
• $L_1 = -G_1 G_2 H_2$   ($\Sigma_2 \to G_1 \to G_2 \to \Sigma_2$)
• $L_2 = -G_3 H_3$   ($\Sigma_3 \to G_3 \to \Sigma_3$)
• $L_3 = -G_1 G_2 G_3$   (Outer unity feedback via main path)
• $L_4 = -G_1 G_4 G_3$   (Outer unity feedback via feedforward $G_4$)
3. Non-Touching Loops Evaluation (Crucial Exam Step!):

Examine loop $L_1$ and loop $L_2$:

• Loop $L_1$ occupies nodes: $\{\Sigma_2, \text{node } X_1, \text{node } X_2\}$
• Loop $L_2$ occupies nodes: $\{\Sigma_3, \text{node } C\}$

$L_1$ and $L_2$ do NOT share any common nodes! They are NON-TOUCHING LOOPS!

$$L_1 L_2 = (-G_1 G_2 H_2) \times (-G_3 H_3) = + G_1 G_2 G_3 H_2 H_3$$

(All other pairs share nodes, so there are no other non-touching pairs, and no triplets).

4. Path Cofactors ($\Delta_k$) & Graph Determinant ($\Delta$):
• Path $P_1$ touches both $L_1$ and $L_2 \implies \mathbf{\Delta_1 = 1}$
• Path $P_2$ touches both $L_1$ (at $X_1$) and $L_2$ (at $\Sigma_3, C$) $\implies \mathbf{\Delta_2 = 1}$
$$\Delta = 1 - (L_1 + L_2 + L_3 + L_4) + L_1 L_2$$ $$= 1 - (-G_1 G_2 H_2 - G_3 H_3 - G_1 G_2 G_3 - G_1 G_4 G_3) + G_1 G_2 G_3 H_2 H_3$$ $$= 1 + G_1 G_2 H_2 + G_3 H_3 + G_1 G_2 G_3 + G_1 G_4 G_3 + G_1 G_2 G_3 H_2 H_3$$
Final Closed-Loop Transfer Function
$$\frac{C(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{G_1 G_3 (G_2 + G_4)}{1 + G_1 G_2 H_2 + G_3 H_3 + G_1 G_3 (G_2 + G_4) + G_1 G_2 G_3 H_2 H_3}$$
JU 2023 Regular Exam • QQ3b [8 Marks • Dual Exam Options]
Block Diagram Reduction vs SFG Mason's Formula

University Examination Question:

“Option 1: Construct the Signal Flow Graph for the system shown in Fig. P-3. Find out the closed loop transfer function of the system using Mason's Gain Formula. (CO3)
Option 2: Find out the ratio $C(s)/R(s)$ for the multiple-loop system shown in Fig. P-3 using block diagram reduction technique. (CO3)”

Method 1: Mason's Gain Formula
Forward Paths:
• $P_1 = G_1 G_2 G_3$   (Touches all loops $\implies \mathbf{\Delta_1 = 1}$)
• $P_2 = G_4$   (Connects $R$ to $C$ directly)
Exam Trap: Path $P_2 = G_4$ touches ONLY nodes $R$ and $C$! None of the 3 feedback loops touch node $R$ or node $C$. Therefore, for $P_2$, all loops are non-touching!
$$\Delta_2 = \Delta$$
Feedback Loops:
• $L_1 = -G_2 H_1$   (Negative feedback to $\Sigma_2$)
• $L_2 = -G_2 G_3 H_2$   (Negative feedback to $\Sigma_2$)
• $L_3 = +G_1 G_2 H_1$   (Positive feedback to $\Sigma_1$!)
Determinant: All loops touch at $G_2 \implies$ no non-touching pairs!
$$\Delta = 1 - (L_1 + L_2 + L_3) = 1 + G_2 H_1 + G_2 G_3 H_2 - G_1 G_2 H_1$$
Method 2: Block Diagram Reduction

Step 1: Superposition Decomposition. Notice that input $R$ enters the main loop at $\Sigma_1$ and also bypasses to $\Sigma_3$ via $G_4$:

$$C(s) = C_{\text{main}}(s) + G_4 R(s)$$

Step 2: Combine Feedbacks at Summer $\Sigma_2$. Summer $\Sigma_2$ receives: $+G_1 E_1$ from input, $-H_1$ from $G_2$ output, and $-G_3 H_2$ from $G_3$ output.

Step 3: Close the loop from $H_1$ to $\Sigma_1$. The feedback $H_1$ returns to $\Sigma_1$ with $+$ sign, passing forward through $G_1$ into $\Sigma_2$ as $+G_1 H_1$.

Step 4: Net loop transfer: The effective forward gain from $\Sigma_1$ to $C$ is $G_1 G_2 G_3$, and the net loop denominator is $1 + G_2 H_1 + G_2 G_3 H_2 - G_1 G_2 H_1$.

Exact Symbolic Closed-Loop Gain (Both Methods Identical)
$$\frac{C(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{G_1 G_2 G_3 + G_4 \Big( 1 + G_2 H_1 + G_2 G_3 H_2 - G_1 G_2 H_1 \Big)}{1 + G_2 H_1 + G_2 G_3 H_2 - G_1 G_2 H_1} = \frac{G_1 G_2 G_3}{\Delta} + G_4$$

1. Canonical System Ratios
Closed-Loop Gain:
T(s) = G(s) / (1 ± G(s)H(s))
Actuating Error Ratio:
E(s)/R(s) = 1 / (1 ± G(s)H(s))
Primary Feedback Ratio:
B(s)/R(s) = GH / (1 ± G(s)H(s))
Disturbance Ratio (plant G2):
CD(s)/D(s) = G2 / (1 + G1G2H)
2. The Shift Duality Mnemonic
Summer Σ
• Along arrow: × G
• Against arrow: × 1/G
Pick-off •
• Along arrow: × 1/G
• Against arrow: × G

Golden Rule: Pick-off movements are the exact mathematical inverse of Summing junction movements!

3. Mason's 4-Line Exam Template
Line 1: List $P_k$ (forward paths).
Line 2: List $L_m$ (all closed loops with signs).
Line 3: Identify non-touching pairs ($\sum L_a L_b$) and compute $\Delta, \Delta_k$.
Line 4: Write $T = \frac{\sum P_k \Delta_k}{\Delta}$.
