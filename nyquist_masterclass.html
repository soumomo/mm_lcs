1. Why Nyquist? The Big Picture & Engineering Motivation

In linear feedback control, the central question is always: Given an open-loop plant $G(s)H(s)$, will the closed-loop system with unity negative feedback be stable?

The closed-loop transfer function is: $$T(s) = \frac{C(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{G(s)}{1 + G(s)H(s)}$$ The poles of the closed-loop system are the roots of the characteristic equation: $$\mathbf{1 + G(s)H(s) = 0 \iff G(s)H(s) = -1 + j0}$$

The Dilemma with Existing Methods:
  • Direct Root Solving: For high-order polynomials ($n \ge 3$), finding roots analytically is tedious and often impossible by hand.
  • Routh-Hurwitz Criterion: Works only on polynomials with constant rational coefficients. It fails completely when the plant contains pure time delay ($e^{-sT}$) or when only experimental frequency response data (magnitude and phase tables) is available.
  • Root Locus: Requires exact pole-zero factorization of the open-loop transfer function.

Harry Nyquist's Breakthrough (1932):
Instead of solving for the roots of $1 + G(s)H(s) = 0$, we test the open-loop frequency response $G(j\omega)H(j\omega)$ along the boundary of the right-half plane. By observing how many times the locus encircles the critical point $-1 + j0$, we can immediately deduce the exact number of unstable closed-loop poles ($Z$)!

2. Cauchy's Principle of Argument: The Geometry of Encirclement

Cauchy's Principle of Argument relates the contour traversal in the $s$-plane to the rotation of phasors in the mapped function plane $F(s)$.

Consider a rational function with $Z$ zeros and $P$ poles: $$F(s) = K \frac{(s - z_1)(s - z_2) \cdots (s - z_Z)}{(s - p_1)(s - p_2) \cdots (s - p_P)}$$ The phase angle of $F(s)$ is the algebraic sum of the angles contributed by all individual factors: $$\mathbf{\angle F(s) = \sum_{i=1}^Z \angle(s - z_i) - \sum_{k=1}^P \angle(s - p_k)}$$

Now let a point $s$ traverse a continuous closed contour $\Gamma_s$ in the clockwise (CW) direction:

Case A: An Enclosed Zero ($z_i$ inside $\Gamma_s$)

As $s$ makes one full clockwise revolution around contour $\Gamma_s$, the vector $(s - z_i)$ rotates by a full circle clockwise: $$\Delta \theta_{z_i} = -360^\circ = -2\pi \text{ radians}$$ Because zeros appear in the numerator (with positive sign in phase), an enclosed zero contributes: $$\Delta \angle F = +1 \times (-2\pi) = \mathbf{-2\pi} \quad \text{(1 Clockwise Rotation of } F\text{)}$$

Case B: An Enclosed Pole ($p_k$ inside $\Gamma_s$)

As $s$ makes one full clockwise revolution, the vector $(s - p_k)$ also rotates by $-360^\circ = -2\pi$ clockwise.
However, because poles reside in the denominator, their phase is subtracted: $$\Delta \angle F = -1 \times (-2\pi) = \mathbf{+2\pi} \quad \text{(1 Counter-Clockwise Rotation of } F\text{)}$$

Case C: An Exterior Singularity (Outside $\Gamma_s$)

If a zero or pole lies outside the contour $\Gamma_s$, as $s$ travels around $\Gamma_s$, the vector to $s$ merely swings back and forth like a pendulum. When $s$ returns to its starting point, the net angle accumulated is strictly $0^\circ$!

Master Cauchy Formula: Over a complete clockwise traversal of $\Gamma_s$, the net phase accumulated by $F(s)$ is: $$\Delta \angle F(s) = 2\pi(P - Z)$$ Dividing by $2\pi$, the net number of counter-clockwise (CCW) encirclements $N_{\text{ccw}}$ of the origin by the locus of $F(s)$ is: $$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z \iff Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}}}$$ (Equivalently, in clockwise direction: $N_{\text{cw}} = Z - P$).
Why the Critical Point is $(-1 + j0)$ instead of $(0 + j0)$

We apply Cauchy's Principle to the characteristic function: $$F(s) = 1 + G(s)H(s)$$ Notice:

  • The zeros of $F(s)$ are roots of $1 + G(s)H(s) = 0$, which are the closed-loop poles ($Z$)!
  • The poles of $F(s)$ are the open-loop poles ($P$) of $G(s)H(s)$!
Therefore, the locus of $1 + G(s)H(s)$ encircles the origin $(0 + j0)$ $N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z$ times.
Subtracting $1$ shifts the entire complex plane to the left by $1$ unit: $$G(s)H(s) = [1 + G(s)H(s)] - 1$$ The origin $(0 + j0)$ of the $F(s)$-plane shifts to the point $-1 + j0$ in the $G(s)H(s)$-plane!
Thus, we directly plot open-loop $G(s)H(s)$ and count encirclements around $(-1 + j0)$.

3. The Canonical Nyquist Contour & Its 4 Segments

To detect all possible unstable closed-loop poles, the contour $\Gamma_s$ must enclose the entire open Right-Half Plane ($\text{Re}(s) > 0$). It consists of 4 distinct continuous paths:

Path C₁ Positive Imaginary Axis ($s = +j\omega$, $\omega \in [0^+, +\infty)$)

Traverses upward from just above the origin to $+j\infty$.
Substituting $s = j\omega$ yields the standard open-loop frequency response $G(j\omega)H(j\omega)$ — this is the familiar Polar Plot!

Path C₂ Infinite Semicircular Arc ($s = R e^{j\theta}$, $R \to \infty$)

Radius $R \to \infty$, sweeping clockwise from $\theta = +90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to -90^\circ$.
For any physical system with more poles than zeros ($n > m$): $$\lim_{R \to \infty} G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K}{s^{n-m}} = \frac{K}{R^{n-m} e^{j(n-m)\theta}} \to 0$$ Path $C_2$ collapses to the exact origin $(0, 0)$ in the $G(s)H(s)$-plane!

Path C₃ Negative Imaginary Axis ($s = -j\omega$, $\omega$ from $-\infty \to 0^-$)

Traverses from $-j\infty$ back toward the origin.
By complex conjugate symmetry: $$G(-j\omega)H(-j\omega) = [G(j\omega)H(j\omega)]^*$$ Path $C_3$ is the exact mirror reflection of Path $C_1$ across the horizontal real axis!

Path C₄ Origin Indentation ($s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}$, $\varepsilon \to 0$)

If the system has poles at the origin ($s = 0$), the contour cannot pass through them (it would make $G(s)H(s)$ singular). We bypass the origin with an infinitesimal semicircle of radius $\varepsilon \to 0$ into the right-half plane, sweeping from $\theta = -90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$ (CCW in $s$-plane).

Behavior in the $G(s)H(s)$-Plane:

  • Type 1 System (Single Pole at Origin, $\frac{1}{s}$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K}{\varepsilon e^{j\theta}} = \left(\frac{K}{\varepsilon}\right) e^{-j\theta} \quad (\text{Radius } \infty)$$ As $\theta$ goes from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$, the angle of $G(s)H(s)$ sweeps from: $$\mathbf{+90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to -90^\circ \quad (180^\circ \text{ Clockwise Arc of Infinite Radius!})}$$
  • Type 2 System (Double Pole at Origin, $\frac{1}{s^2}$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K}{\varepsilon^2 e^{j2\theta}} = \left(\frac{K}{\varepsilon^2}\right) e^{-j2\theta} \quad (\text{Radius } \infty)$$ As $\theta$ goes from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$, the angle sweeps: $$\mathbf{+180^\circ \to 0^\circ \to -180^\circ \quad (360^\circ \text{ Full Circle of Infinite Radius!})}$$

4. How to Count Encirclements $N$ Without Error

Many students lose marks because they get confused about whether an encirclement is clockwise or counter-clockwise. Here is the foolproof Ray-Tracing Method taught by Prof. Madhubanti Maitra:

The Ray-Tracing Rule
  1. Place a pencil at the critical point $-1 + j0$.
  2. Draw an imaginary ray from $-1 + j0$ extending out to infinity in any convenient direction (usually along the negative real axis to the left, or vertically upward).
  3. Observe every point where the Nyquist locus crosses this ray:
    • If the curve crosses from right to left (counter-clockwise orientation relative to $-1+j0$), count $+1$.
    • If the curve crosses from left to right (clockwise orientation), count $-1$.
  4. The algebraic sum of all crossings equals the net counter-clockwise encirclements: $\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}}}$.
The Universal Stability Equation: $$\mathbf{Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}}}$$
  • $Z = 0$ $\implies$ System is STABLE.
  • $Z > 0$ $\implies$ System is UNSTABLE with $Z$ closed-loop poles in the right-half plane.
  • $Z < 0$ $\implies$ Mathematical contradiction! You counted $N$ or $P$ incorrectly.

6. The 3 Master University Examination Archetypes

Archetype 1: Open-Loop Unstable Plant with $P=1$ [JU 2024 Supplementary Q3, 16 Marks]

Transfer Function: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s-1)}$$

  • Open-Loop RHP Poles: Root at $s = +1 \implies \mathbf{P = 1}$.
  • Frequency Response on $s=j\omega$: $$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = -\frac{2}{1+\omega^2} - j \frac{\omega}{1+\omega^2}$$ Both real and imaginary parts are strictly negative for $\omega > 0$. The locus lies entirely in the 3rd quadrant!
  • Negative Real-Axis Intercept: At $\omega = 0$, $G(j0)H(j0) = -2 + j0$.
  • Encirclement of Critical Point $(-1+j0)$: Since $-2 < -1$, the closed contour encompasses $(-1+j0)$ in the counter-clockwise direction: $$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = +1}$$
  • Stability: $Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 1 = \mathbf{0}$. The closed-loop system is STABLE!
Archetype 2: Plant with Origin Pole & RHP Pole [Class Test 2024-25 Q4 • JU 2023, 12 Marks]

Transfer Function: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$$

  • Open-Loop RHP Poles: Root at $s = +1 \implies \mathbf{P = 1}$.
  • Origin Indentation ($s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}$, $\theta: -90^\circ \to +90^\circ$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{3K}{\varepsilon e^{j\theta}(-1)} = \left(\frac{3K}{\varepsilon}\right) e^{j(180^\circ - \theta)}$$ Sweeps from $+270^\circ \to 180^\circ \to +90^\circ$ (a $180^\circ$ clockwise arc through the left-half plane!).
  • Real-Axis Intercept: Solving $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = 0$ yields $\omega_{pc} = \sqrt{3}\text{ rad/s}$. $$\text{Re}[G(j\sqrt{3})H(j\sqrt{3})] = -K$$
  • Stability Condition: For stability with $P=1$, we must have $N_{\text{ccw}} = 1$. The locus encircles $-1+j0$ counter-clockwise if and only if the intercept $-K$ lies to the left of $-1$: $$-K < -1 \iff \mathbf{K > 1}$$
  • Conclusion: Closed-loop system is STABLE for $K > 1$; UNSTABLE for $K < 1$.
Archetype 3: Type 1 Third-Order System [JU 2017 Q7, 16 Marks]

Transfer Function: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{1}{s(2s+1)(s+1)}$$

  • Open-Loop RHP Poles: $P = 0$ (All poles at $s=0, -0.5, -1$ are in LHP or origin).
  • Phase Crossover Frequency: $$\angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(2\omega) - \tan^{-1}(\omega) = -180^\circ \implies \mathbf{\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707\text{ rad/s}}$$
  • Magnitude at $\omega_{pc}$: $$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \sqrt{1 + 2} \cdot \sqrt{1 + 0.5}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{1.5}} = \frac{1}{1.5} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3} \approx 0.667}$$ The locus crosses the negative real axis at coordinate $\mathbf{-0.667 + j0}$.
  • Stability: Since $-0.667 > -1$, the critical point $(-1+j0)$ is NOT enclosed! $$N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 - 0 = \mathbf{0}$$ The closed-loop system is strictly STABLE!

7. Ascertaining Gain Margin and Phase Margin from the Open-Loop Polar Plot

In examination papers (e.g. JU 2024 Supplementary Q5(iii) for 8 Marks), students are asked to explain how Gain Margin and Phase Margin are ascertained directly from the polar plot.

1. Gain Crossover Frequency ($\omega_{gc}$) & Phase Margin ($PM$)
  • Definition of $\omega_{gc}$: The frequency at which the open-loop magnitude equals unity ($1.0$): $$|G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})| = 1.0$$ Graphical Representation: It is the intersection point of the polar plot with the Unit Circle centered at the origin.
  • Phase Margin ($PM$): The additional phase lag that can be introduced into the system at $\omega_{gc}$ before it becomes unstable: $$\mathbf{PM = 180^\circ + \angle G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})}$$ Graphical Measurement: Draw a radial line from the origin to the unit-circle intersection point. The angle measured counter-clockwise from the negative real axis ($-180^\circ$) to this line is the Phase Margin.
2. Phase Crossover Frequency ($\omega_{pc}$) & Gain Margin ($GM$)
  • Definition of $\omega_{pc}$: The frequency at which the open-loop phase angle is $-180^\circ$: $$\angle G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ \iff \text{Im}[G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})] = 0$$ Graphical Representation: It is the intersection point of the polar plot with the negative real axis. Let the distance from the origin to this point be $a = |G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|$.
  • Gain Margin ($GM$): The reciprocal of the magnitude at the phase crossover frequency: $$\mathbf{GM = \frac{1}{|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|} = \frac{1}{a}}$$ $$\mathbf{GM_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(GM) = -20 \log_{10}(a)}$$
Stability Criterion from Margins: For an open-loop stable, minimum-phase system:
  • Stable: $\omega_{gc} < \omega_{pc} \implies GM > 1\text{ (positive in dB)}$ and $PM > 0^\circ$.
  • Marginally Stable: $\omega_{gc} = \omega_{pc} \implies GM = 1\text{ (0 dB)}$ and $PM = 0^\circ$.
  • Unstable: $\omega_{gc} > \omega_{pc} \implies GM < 1\text{ (negative in dB)}$ and $PM < 0^\circ$.
