Mathematical Formulation of Evans Angle and Magnitude Criteria

State the basic conditions under which a point in the complex $s$-plane lies on the root locus of a linear feedback control system, and show how the open-loop pole-zero vectors govern closed-loop root migration.
1. Closed-Loop Characteristic Equation

Consider a standard single-loop negative feedback system with forward path transfer function $G(s)$ and feedback path transfer function $H(s)$. The closed-loop transfer function is given by: $$T(s) = \frac{C(s)}{R(s)} = \frac{G(s)}{1 + G(s)H(s)}$$ The closed-loop poles are the roots of the characteristic equation: $$1 + G(s)H(s) = 0 \iff G(s)H(s) = -1 = 1 \cdot e^{j(2q+1)\pi}, \quad q \in \{0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots\}$$ Expressing the open-loop transfer function in factored pole-zero form with adjustable loop gain parameter $K \ge 0$: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K \prod_{i=1}^m (s + z_i)}{\prod_{j=1}^n (s + p_j)} = \frac{K \prod_{i=1}^m (s - z_i')}{\prod_{j=1}^n (s - p_j')}$$ where $-z_i$ are the $m$ open-loop zeros and $-p_j$ are the $n$ open-loop poles ($n \ge m$ for physical causality).

2. The Evans Angle Criterion (Spatial Geometry Condition)

Equating the phase angle of both sides of $G(s)H(s) = -1$: $$\angle [G(s)H(s)] = \pm 180^\circ (2q + 1) = \pm (2q + 1)\pi \text{ rad}, \quad q = 0, 1, 2, \dots$$ In terms of individual vector angles drawn from the open-loop zeros ($\theta_{zi} = \angle(s + z_i)$) and open-loop poles ($\theta_{pj} = \angle(s + p_j)$) to an arbitrary test point $s$: $$\sum_{i=1}^m \angle(s + z_i) - \sum_{j=1}^n \angle(s + p_j) = \pm 180^\circ(2q + 1)$$ $$\sum_{i=1}^m \theta_{zi} - \sum_{j=1}^n \theta_{pj} = \pm 180^\circ(2q + 1)$$ Crucial Theoretical Implication: The Angle Criterion is completely independent of the scalar gain $K$. It is the sole condition that determines whether a candidate point $s$ belongs to the root locus.

3. The Evans Magnitude Criterion (Gain Calibration Condition)

Equating the absolute magnitudes of both sides of $G(s)H(s) = -1$: $$|G(s)H(s)| = 1 \implies \frac{K \prod_{i=1}^m |s + z_i|}{\prod_{j=1}^n |s + p_j|} = 1$$ Solving explicitly for the parameter $K$: $$K = \frac{\prod_{j=1}^n |s + p_j|}{\prod_{i=1}^m |s + z_i|} = \frac{\prod_{j=1}^n (\text{length of vector from pole } p_j \text{ to } s)}{\prod_{i=1}^m (\text{length of vector from zero } z_i \text{ to } s)}$$ Crucial Theoretical Implication: The Magnitude Criterion cannot be used to determine locus trajectories; it is evaluated strictly at points that already satisfy the Angle Criterion to compute the critical gain $K$ required to position closed-loop poles at those specific locations.

Examiner Note • Rigorous Convention In standard Evans root locus analysis, negative feedback is assumed ($1 + G(s)H(s) = 0$), leading to the odd-multiple of $180^\circ$ angle criterion ($180^\circ$ locus). For positive feedback systems ($1 - G(s)H(s) = 0$), the condition becomes $\angle G(s)H(s) = \pm q \cdot 360^\circ$ ($0^\circ$ locus).

Exact Circular Root Locus Proof for Second-Order System with a Zero

For a unity feedback control system with open-loop transfer function $$G(s) = \frac{K(s + b)}{s(s + a)}, \quad |a| < |b| \quad (b > a > 0)$$ using the Evans Angle Criterion, rigorously prove that the complex branches of the root locus form a circle centered at $(-b, 0)$ with radius $R = \sqrt{b^2 - ab}$. Determine the exact coordinates of the breakaway and break-in points, verify them via $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$, and derive the analytical expression for the minimum damping ratio $\zeta_{\min} = \sqrt{a/b}$.
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularities & Real-Axis Geometry

We are given the open-loop transfer function with negative unity feedback ($H(s) = 1$): $$G(s) = \frac{K(s + b)}{s(s + a)}, \quad b > a > 0$$ Let us identify all finite open-loop singularities:

  • Open-Loop Poles ($n = 2$): Setting the denominator $s(s + a) = 0$ yields two poles at $s = 0$ and $s = -a$.
  • Open-Loop Zero ($m = 1$): Setting the numerator $s + b = 0$ yields one finite zero at $s = -b$.

Since $b > a > 0$, the numerical positions along the negative real axis from right to left are: $$\text{Origin } 0 > -a > -b$$ Therefore, the zero at $-b$ lies strictly to the left of the pole at $-a$.

Real-Axis Root Locus Criterion: A point on the real axis belongs to the root locus if and only if the total number of real poles and zeros lying to its right is odd.

  • For segment $[-a, 0]$: 1 pole to the right ($s = 0$, odd) $\implies$ Locus exists on $[-a, 0]$.
  • For segment $[-b, -a]$: 2 singularities to the right ($s = 0, -a$, even) $\implies$ No locus on $[-b, -a]$.
  • For segment $(-\infty, -b]$: 3 singularities to the right ($s = 0, -a, -b$, odd) $\implies$ Locus exists on $(-\infty, -b]$.
Step 2: Characteristic Equation & Evans Angle Criterion

The closed-loop characteristic equation is: $$1 + G(s)H(s) = 0 \implies 1 + \frac{K(s + b)}{s(s + a)} = 0 \implies s(s + a) + K(s + b) = 0$$ For any point $s$ in the complex plane to lie on the root locus for positive real gain ($K > 0$), it must satisfy Evans' Angle Criterion: $$\sum \angle(\text{zeros to } s) - \sum \angle(\text{poles to } s) = (2q + 1)180^\circ, \quad q \in \mathbb{Z}$$ Substituting the one open-loop zero at $-b$ and the two open-loop poles at $0$ and $-a$, and selecting the principal odd multiple $-180^\circ$ ($-\pi\text{ rad}$): $$\angle(s + b) - \angle(s) - \angle(s + a) = -\pi$$

Step 3: Substitution of Rectangular Coordinates $s = \sigma + j\omega$

Represent the complex eigenvalue in rectangular coordinates as $s = \sigma + j\omega$, where $\sigma$ is the real part (attenuation factor) and $\omega$ is the imaginary part (oscillation frequency):

  • $s + b = (\sigma + b) + j\omega \implies \angle(s + b) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + b}\right)$
  • $s = \sigma + j\omega \implies \angle(s) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma}\right)$
  • $s + a = (\sigma + a) + j\omega \implies \angle(s + a) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + a}\right)$

Substituting these phase angles into the Evans Angle Criterion: $$\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + b}\right) - \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma}\right) - \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + a}\right) = -\pi$$ Transposing the two pole phase angles to the left-hand side and moving $\pi$ yields: $$\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma}\right) + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + a}\right) = \pi + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + b}\right)$$

Step 4: Application of the Tangent Addition Formula

Define the three component angles: $$A = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma}\right), \quad B = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + a}\right), \quad C = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + b}\right)$$ The angle balance relation is: $$A + B = \pi + C$$ Taking the tangent of both sides: $$\tan(A + B) = \tan(\pi + C)$$ Since the tangent function has a fundamental period of $\pi$ radians ($\tan(\pi + C) = \tan C$), and applying the standard tangent addition formula $\tan(A + B) = \frac{\tan A + \tan B}{1 - \tan A \tan B}$: $$\frac{\frac{\omega}{\sigma} + \frac{\omega}{\sigma + a}}{1 - \left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma}\right)\left(\frac{\omega}{\sigma + a}\right)} = \frac{\omega}{\sigma + b}$$ Simplifying the numerator on the LHS by finding a common denominator $\sigma(\sigma + a)$: $$\text{Numerator} = \frac{\omega(\sigma + a) + \omega\sigma}{\sigma(\sigma + a)} = \frac{\omega(2\sigma + a)}{\sigma(\sigma + a)}$$ Simplifying the denominator on the LHS: $$\text{Denominator} = \frac{\sigma(\sigma + a) - \omega^2}{\sigma(\sigma + a)}$$ Cancelling the identical common denominator $\sigma(\sigma + a)$: $$\frac{\omega(2\sigma + a)}{\sigma(\sigma + a) - \omega^2} = \frac{\omega}{\sigma + b}$$

Step 5: Isolating Complex Branches ($\omega \neq 0$) & Completing the Square

For the complex branches of the root locus that depart into the $s$-plane, $\omega \neq 0$. Dividing both sides of the equation by $\omega$: $$\frac{2\sigma + a}{\sigma(\sigma + a) - \omega^2} = \frac{1}{\sigma + b}$$ Cross-multiplying both sides: $$(2\sigma + a)(\sigma + b) = \sigma(\sigma + a) - \omega^2$$ Expanding both polynomial products: $$2\sigma^2 + 2b\sigma + a\sigma + ab = \sigma^2 + a\sigma - \omega^2$$ Subtracting $\sigma^2 + a\sigma$ from both sides: $$\sigma^2 + 2b\sigma + ab = -\omega^2 \implies \sigma^2 + 2b\sigma + \omega^2 = -ab$$ To obtain the standard equation of a circle, we complete the square in $\sigma$ by adding $b^2$ to both sides: $$\sigma^2 + 2b\sigma + b^2 + \omega^2 = b^2 - ab$$ $$(\sigma + b)^2 + \omega^2 = b^2 - ab \tag{Circle Equation}$$ Comparing this directly with the standard Cartesian equation of a circle $(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 = R^2$:

Centre $C$: $(-b, 0)$ • Exactly at the open-loop zero location Radius $R$: $\sqrt{b^2 - ab} = \sqrt{b(b - a)}$ Existence Condition: Since $b > a > 0 \implies b(b - a) > 0$, radius $R$ is strictly real
Step 6: Analytical Determination of Breakaway and Break-in Points

A fundamental question in root locus construction is: Where does this circular trajectory meet the real axis?

On the real axis, the imaginary component is strictly zero ($\omega = 0$). Substituting $\omega = 0$ directly into our proven circle equation: $$(\sigma + b)^2 + 0 = b^2 - ab \implies (\sigma + b)^2 = b^2 - ab$$ Taking the square root on both sides: $$\sigma + b = \pm \sqrt{b^2 - ab} \implies \sigma = -b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - ab}$$ This yields two distinct real-axis intersection points:

  1. Breakaway Point ($\sigma_{\text{breakaway}}$): $$\sigma_1 = -b + \sqrt{b^2 - ab}$$ Physical Trajectory: Since $b > a > 0$, we have $0 < \sqrt{b(b-a)} < b$. Furthermore, $\sqrt{b^2 - ab} > b - a$, which guarantees: $$-a < \sigma_1 < 0$$ $\sigma_1$ lies strictly on the active real-axis locus segment between open-loop poles $0$ and $-a$. The two branches originating at $s = 0$ and $s = -a$ move towards each other as gain $K$ increases, collide at $\sigma_1$, form a double root, and break away from the real axis at angles of $\pm 90^\circ$ into the complex plane to trace the circle.
  2. Break-in Point ($\sigma_{\text{break-in}}$): $$\sigma_2 = -b - \sqrt{b^2 - ab}$$ Physical Trajectory: Since $\sqrt{b^2 - ab} > 0$, we have $\sigma_2 < -b$. Therefore, $\sigma_2$ lies strictly to the left of the finite zero on the active real-axis segment $(-\infty, -b]$. The two complex conjugate circular branches traverse the upper and lower semicircles, return to the real axis as $K$ increases further, collide at $\sigma_2$, and break into the real axis at angles of $\pm 90^\circ$.

    Upon breaking in at $\sigma_2$:
    • One branch moves to the right along the real axis and terminates on the finite open-loop zero at $s = -b$ as $K \to \infty$.
    • The other branch moves to the left along the real axis toward $-\infty$ (the zero at infinity) as $K \to \infty$.
Algebraic Cross-Verification via the $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ Criterion:
From the closed-loop characteristic equation $s(s + a) + K(s + b) = 0$, isolate the gain $K$: $$K = -\frac{s(s + a)}{s + b} = -\frac{s^2 + as}{s + b}$$ Differentiating with respect to $s$ via the quotient rule and setting $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$: $$\frac{dK}{ds} = -\frac{(2s + a)(s + b) - (s^2 + as)(1)}{(s + b)^2} = 0$$ $$(2s^2 + 2bs + as + ab) - (s^2 + as) = 0 \implies s^2 + 2bs + ab = 0$$ Applying the quadratic formula to solve for $s$: $$s = \frac{-2b \pm \sqrt{(2b)^2 - 4(1)(ab)}}{2(1)} = \frac{-2b \pm \sqrt{4(b^2 - ab)}}{2} = -b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - ab}$$ Conclusion: The roots of $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ match the circle's real-axis intercepts with 100% mathematical identity! This rigorously confirms that the breakaway and break-in points are the entry and exit gates of the circular locus.
Step 7: Geometric Derivation of Minimum Damping Ratio $\zeta_{\min} = \sqrt{a/b}$

For an underdamped complex conjugate pole pair $s = -\zeta\omega_n \pm j\omega_n\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$, the damping ratio is given geometrically by: $$\zeta = \cos\theta$$ where $\theta$ is the angle subtended between the negative real axis and the radial line connecting the origin $O(0,0)$ to the closed-loop pole location $P(\sigma, \omega)$.

To find the minimum damping ratio $\zeta_{\min}$, the angle $\theta$ must be maximized ($\cos\theta$ is a strictly decreasing function of $\theta$ in the first quadrant $0 \le \theta \le 90^\circ$). On a circular path lying strictly in the left-half $s$-plane, the radial line from the origin reaches its maximum angle $\theta_{\max}$ when the line $OP$ is tangent to the circle at point $P$!

Consider the right-angled triangle $\triangle OCP$ formed by:

  • Origin $O(0, 0)$: Distance along the real axis to circle centre is $OC = b$.
  • Circle Centre $C(-b, 0)$: Located at the zero on the negative real axis.
  • Point of Tangency $P$: Lies on the circular locus, so radius $CP = R = \sqrt{b^2 - ab}$.

Fundamental Geometric Theorem: The radius $CP$ drawn to the point of tangency is strictly perpendicular to the tangent line $OP$ ($CP \perp OP$). Therefore, triangle $\triangle OCP$ is a right-angled triangle with the right angle at $P$ ($\angle CPO = 90^\circ$).

Applying the Pythagorean Theorem to right-angled triangle $\triangle OCP$: $$OC^2 = OP^2 + CP^2 \implies b^2 = OP^2 + (b^2 - ab)$$ $$OP^2 = b^2 - (b^2 - ab) = ab \implies OP = \sqrt{ab}$$ In the right-angled triangle $\triangle OCP$, the angle $\angle COP$ between the negative real axis (segment $OC$) and the tangent line $OP$ is $\theta$: $$\cos\theta = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{OP}{OC}$$ Substituting $OP = \sqrt{ab}$ and $OC = b$: $$\zeta_{\min} = \frac{\sqrt{ab}}{b} = \sqrt{\frac{ab}{b^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} \tag{Minimum Damping Ratio}$$

Step 8: Master Examination Summary
Circle Equation: $(\sigma + b)^2 + \omega^2 = b^2 - ab$ Centre: $(-b, 0)$ Radius: $R = \sqrt{b^2 - ab}$ Breakaway Point: $\sigma_1 = -b + \sqrt{b^2 - ab} \in (-a, 0)$ Break-in Point: $\sigma_2 = -b - \sqrt{b^2 - ab} \in (-\infty, -b)$ Tangent Length: $OP = \sqrt{ab}$ Minimum Damping Ratio: $\zeta_{\min} = \sqrt{a/b}$

Core Analytical Mechanics • Real Axis Extrema

Theoretical Significance of the Condition $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ on the Real Axis

Explain the mathematical and physical significance of the equation $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ on the real axis. Clarify why it represents coincident closed-loop poles, distinguish between breakaway and break-in points, explain root sensitivity, and outline the necessary-versus-sufficient validity filters.
1. Mathematical Origin: Condition for Multiple (Coincident) Roots

Let the open-loop transfer function be expressed as a ratio of coprime polynomials $G(s)H(s) = \frac{A(s)}{B(s)}$, where $A(s) = \prod_{i=1}^m (s + z_i)$ and $B(s) = \prod_{j=1}^n (s + p_j)$. The closed-loop characteristic equation is: $$F(s, K) = B(s) + K A(s) = 0 \implies K(s) = -\frac{B(s)}{A(s)}$$ From classical algebra, a polynomial equation $F(s) = 0$ possesses a multiple (coincident) root of multiplicity $r \ge 2$ at $s = s_b$ if and only if both the function itself and its first derivative vanish simultaneously: $$F(s_b) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\partial F}{\partial s}\Bigg|_{s = s_b} = 0$$ Differentiating $F(s, K) = B(s) + K A(s) = 0$ partially with respect to $s$: $$\frac{\partial F}{\partial s} = B'(s) + K A'(s) = 0 \implies K = -\frac{B'(s)}{A'(s)}$$ Equating the two expressions for $K$: $$-\frac{B(s)}{A(s)} = -\frac{B'(s)}{A'(s)} \implies B'(s)A(s) - B(s)A'(s) = 0$$ Now evaluate the total derivative of $K(s) = -\frac{B(s)}{A(s)}$ using the calculus quotient rule: $$\frac{dK}{ds} = -\frac{B'(s)A(s) - B(s)A'(s)}{[A(s)]^2} = 0 \iff B'(s)A(s) - B(s)A'(s) = 0$$ Fundamental Mathematical Conclusion: The equation $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ is the exact analytical condition for the closed-loop characteristic polynomial to possess repeated, coincident roots in the complex $s$-plane.

2. Physical & Geometric Distinction: Breakaway vs. Break-in Points

When restricted to the real axis ($s = \sigma \in \mathbb{R}$), $K(\sigma) = -\frac{B(\sigma)}{A(\sigma)}$ represents a real-valued scalar profile. The stationary points where $\frac{dK}{d\sigma} = 0$ demarcate two distinct physical phenomena:

Attribute Breakaway Point Break-in Point
Geometric Definition Local Maximum of $K(\sigma)$ on locus segment ($\frac{d^2K}{d\sigma^2} < 0$) Local Minimum of $K(\sigma)$ on locus segment ($\frac{d^2K}{d\sigma^2} > 0$)
Topological Bounding Bounded by two adjacent open-loop poles Bounded by two open-loop zeros (or zero and $-\infty$)
Root Motion Trajectory Real roots travel toward each other as $K \uparrow$, collide at $K_{\max}$, and depart into complex plane Complex conjugate roots travel toward real axis as $K \uparrow$, arrive at $K_{\min}$, and split along real axis
Number of Real Roots For $K > K_{\max}$, zero real roots remain on that segment For $K > K_{\min}$, two distinct real roots exist on that segment
3. Root Sensitivity Mechanics ($S_K^s \to \infty$)

The parametric sensitivity of closed-loop pole positions $s$ with respect to controller gain $K$ is defined by: $$S_K^s = \frac{ds}{dK} = \frac{1}{\frac{dK}{ds}}$$ At any point where $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$, the root sensitivity becomes unbounded: $$\lim_{\frac{dK}{ds} \to 0} \left|\frac{ds}{dK}\right| = \infty$$ Geometric Consequence: An infinitesimal increment $\Delta K$ in controller gain produces an infinitely fast rate of displacement of the closed-loop poles. For $r$ colliding branches, the angle of departure/arrival is given by: $$\theta = \pm \frac{180^\circ}{r}$$ For the universal case of two colliding branches ($r = 2$), the branches depart or arrive at exactly $\pm 90^\circ$, perpendicular to the real axis.

4. Validity Filters: Necessary vs. Sufficient Condition

The equation $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ is a necessary condition, but not a sufficient condition. A real root $\sigma_b$ of $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ represents a physical breakaway/break-in point if and only if it simultaneously satisfies two strict validation tests:

Test 1 • Locus Segment Inclusion (Angle Criterion Filter):
The candidate point $\sigma_b$ must lie on an active real-axis segment of the root locus. By Rule 3, the total count of open-loop poles and open-loop zeros on the real axis to the right of $\sigma_b$ must be an odd integer.

Test 2 • Gain Positivity (Magnitude Criterion Filter):
The physical controller gain evaluated at that coordinate must be strictly positive and real: $$K(\sigma_b) = -\frac{B(\sigma_b)}{A(\sigma_b)} > 0$$

The Classic Examiner Trap Any root of $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$ that yields $K < 0$, or yields complex values of $K$, or falls on a real-axis segment with an even number of singularities to its right, is an extraneous algebraic artifact of polynomial differentiation and must be explicitly discarded in examination scripts!

Determination and Rigorous Justification of Breakaway Points for $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+3)(s^2 + 2s + 2)}$

Consider the following unity feedback system with open-loop transfer function: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+3)(s^2 + 2s + 2)}$$ Determine the breakaway point or points. Justify your answer thoroughly. [06 Marks]
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularities & Real-Axis Root Locus Segment

Factoring the denominator of $G(s)H(s)$:
• Real poles at: $s = 0$ and $s = -3$
• Complex conjugate poles from $s^2 + 2s + 2 = 0 \implies s = -1 \pm j1$
• Finite zeros: $m = 0$

By Evans' Rule 3, a point on the real axis lies on the root locus if and only if the number of real poles and zeros to its right is odd. Complex conjugate poles contribute zero net angle ($360^\circ$) to any point on the real axis.
Singularities on real axis from right to left: $s = 0$ and $s = -3$.
• For $\sigma > 0$: 0 singularities to right (Even) $\implies$ No locus.
• For $-3 < \sigma < 0$: 1 singularity ($s=0$) to right (Odd) $\implies$ ON ROOT LOCUS!
• For $\sigma < -3$: 2 singularities ($0$ and $-3$) to right (Even) $\implies$ No locus.
Hence, the root locus on the real axis exists strictly on the interval $[-3, 0]$.

Step 2: Formulate Characteristic Equation for Loop Gain $K$

With unity feedback $H(s) = 1$: $$1 + G(s)H(s) = 0 \implies 1 + \frac{K}{s(s+3)(s^2 + 2s + 2)} = 0$$ $$K(s) = -s(s+3)(s^2 + 2s + 2) = -(s^2 + 3s)(s^2 + 2s + 2)$$ Expanding the polynomial product: $$K(s) = -(s^4 + 2s^3 + 2s^2 + 3s^3 + 6s^2 + 6s) = -(s^4 + 5s^3 + 8s^2 + 6s)$$

Step 3: Differentiate to Establish Stationary Condition $\frac{dK}{ds} = 0$

Taking the first derivative of $K(s)$ with respect to $s$: $$\frac{dK}{ds} = -\frac{d}{ds}(s^4 + 5s^3 + 8s^2 + 6s) = -(4s^3 + 15s^2 + 16s + 6) = 0$$ $$4s^3 + 15s^2 + 16s + 6 = 0$$ Solving this cubic equation yields one real root and two complex conjugate roots: $$\begin{cases} s_1 = -2.2886 \approx \mathbf{-2.29} \\ s_{2,3} = -0.7307 \pm j0.3486 \approx \mathbf{-0.73 \pm j0.35} \end{cases}$$

Step 4: Physical vs. Extraneous Root Justification

Validation of Real Root $s_1 = -2.29$:
1. Segment Test: $-3 < -2.2886 < 0$. The root lies squarely within the active real-axis root locus segment $[-3, 0]$.
2. Gain Positivity Test: $$K(-2.2886) = -[(-2.2886)^4 + 5(-2.2886)^3 + 8(-2.2886)^2 + 6(-2.2886)] = \mathbf{+4.3316 > 0}$$ Since $s_1$ belongs to the root locus and requires a strictly positive, real loop gain $K = 4.33$, $s_1 = -2.29$ is a genuine physical breakaway point.

Rejection of Complex Conjugate Roots $s_{2,3} = -0.73 \pm j0.35$:
1. Non-Real Gain: Evaluating $K(s)$ at $s = -0.7307 + j0.3486$: $$K = 1.7932 - j0.1759 \notin \mathbb{R}$$ On an authentic Evans root locus, the gain parameter $K$ must be strictly real and positive ($K \in \mathbb{R}^+$).
2. Angle Criterion Violation: The net phase angle $\angle G(s)H(s)$ at $s_{2,3}$ does not equal $\pm 180^\circ$.
Consequently, $s_{2,3}$ are purely extraneous algebraic artifacts and do not correspond to physical breakaway points.

Official Examination Conclusion The system possesses exactly one valid breakaway point located on the real axis at $\mathbf{s = -2.29}$, occurring at the critical breakaway gain $\mathbf{K = 4.33}$.

Break-in Point and Complex Pole Departure Analysis for $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+2)}{s^2 + 2s + 2}$

For a unity feedback control system with: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s + 2)}{s^2 + 2s + 2}$$ calculate the exact break-in point on the real axis, verify it with the Angle Criterion, compute the angle of departure from the complex conjugate poles, and sketch the locus trajectory.
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularities & Real Axis Segment

Open-loop poles: Roots of $s^2 + 2s + 2 = 0 \implies s = -1 \pm j1$ ($n = 2$).
Open-loop zero: $s = -2$ ($m = 1$).
Number of branches: $N = \max(2, 1) = 2$.
Real axis segment: Singularities on the real axis comprise only the zero at $s = -2$. Points to the left of $-2$ have 1 real singularity to their right (odd) $\implies$ Root locus exists along $(-\infty, -2]$.

Step 2: Analytical Calculation of Break-in Point

Closed-loop characteristic equation: $$1 + \frac{K(s + 2)}{s^2 + 2s + 2} = 0 \implies K(s) = -\frac{s^2 + 2s + 2}{s + 2}$$ Differentiating with respect to $s$ using the quotient rule: $$\frac{dK}{ds} = -\frac{(2s + 2)(s + 2) - (s^2 + 2s + 2)(1)}{(s + 2)^2} = -\frac{(2s^2 + 6s + 4) - (s^2 + 2s + 2)}{(s + 2)^2} = -\frac{s^2 + 4s + 2}{(s + 2)^2} = 0$$ Setting the numerator polynomial to zero: $$s^2 + 4s + 2 = 0 \implies s = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 8}}{2} = -2 \pm \sqrt{2}$$ $$\mathbf{s_1 = -2 + \sqrt{2} \approx -0.586}, \quad \mathbf{s_2 = -2 - \sqrt{2} \approx -3.414}$$

Step 3: Rigorous Validation Filter

• Candidate $s_1 = -0.586$: Lies to the right of the zero $s = -2$. There are zero real singularities to its right (count = 0, even) $\implies$ Off root locus! Discarded!
• Candidate $s_2 = -3.414$: Lies in the interval $(-\infty, -2]$, having 1 real zero to its right (count = 1, odd) $\implies$ Valid Break-in Point!
Evaluating the loop gain at the break-in point: $$K(-3.414) = -\frac{(-3.414)^2 + 2(-3.414) + 2}{-3.414 + 2} = -\frac{11.655 - 6.828 + 2}{-1.414} = \frac{6.827}{1.414} = \mathbf{+4.828 > 0}$$ Because $K > 0$ and $\frac{d^2K}{d\sigma^2} > 0$, this point is a genuine physical break-in point (local gain minimum).

Step 4: Angle of Departure from Complex Poles

Consider the complex pole at $p_1 = -1 + j1$.
• Vector angle from open-loop zero at $-2$: $$\theta_z = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{-1 - (-2)}\right) = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$$
• Vector angle from complex conjugate pole at $-1 - j1$: $$\theta_p = 90^\circ$$ By the Evans Angle Criterion: $$\phi_d = 180^\circ - \sum \theta_{\text{poles}} + \sum \theta_{\text{zeros}} = 180^\circ - 90^\circ + 45^\circ = \mathbf{135^\circ}$$ By symmetry, the angle of departure from $-1 - j1$ is $-135^\circ$.

Complete Root Locus Construction for $q(s) = s^4 + 3s^3 + 12s^2 + (K - 16)s + K = 0$

Plot the Root-Locus for a unity feedback system whose characteristic equation is: $$q(s) = s^4 + 3s^3 + 12s^2 + (K - 16)s + K = 0; \quad K \ge 0$$ Also determine and indicate on the sketch:
(i) number and angles of asymptotes,
(ii) the centroid,
(iii) the breakaway point / points, if any,
(iv) intersection of the root locus and the asymptotes with the imaginary axis, if any,
(v) the range of gain $K$ for which the closed loop system remains stable,
(vi) any other value that has relevance to the plotting of root locus. [12 Marks]
Step 1: Convert to Canonical Evans Form $1 + K G_0(s) = 0$

Isolate the terms independent of $K$ from the terms linear in $K$: $$(s^4 + 3s^3 + 12s^2 - 16s) + K(s + 1) = 0$$ Dividing by $s^4 + 3s^3 + 12s^2 - 16s$: $$1 + \frac{K(s + 1)}{s(s^3 + 3s^2 + 12s - 16)} = 0$$ Factoring the cubic term: Testing $s = 1 \implies 1^3 + 3(1)^2 + 12(1) - 16 = 0$. By synthetic division: $$s^3 + 3s^2 + 12s - 16 = (s - 1)(s^2 + 4s + 16)$$ Roots of $s^2 + 4s + 16 = 0$: $s = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 64}}{2} = -2 \pm j\sqrt{12} = -2 \pm j2\sqrt{3} \approx -2 \pm j3.464$.

Open-loop Poles ($n=4$): $s = 0, \quad \mathbf{+1\text{ (RHP Unstable Pole!)}}, \quad -2 \pm j2\sqrt{3}$ Open-loop Zero ($m=1$): $s = -1$ Locus Branches: $N = \max(4, 1) = 4$
Step 2: Real Axis Segments (Rule 3)

Singularities on the real axis from right to left: $+1$ (pole), $0$ (pole), $-1$ (zero).
• $\sigma > +1$: 0 singularities to right (Even) $\implies$ No locus.
• $0 < \sigma < +1$: 1 singularity ($+1$) to right (Odd) $\implies$ ON ROOT LOCUS! Branches originate at $s=0$ and $s=+1$ moving toward each other.
• $-1 < \sigma < 0$: 2 singularities ($+1, 0$) to right (Even) $\implies$ No locus.
• $\sigma < -1$: 3 singularities ($+1, 0, -1$) to right (Odd) $\implies$ ON ROOT LOCUS! Locus extends from $-1$ to $-\infty$.

Step 3: Asymptotes and Centroid (Rules 4 & 5)

Number of branches terminating at infinity: $n - m = 4 - 1 = \mathbf{3}$.
Angles of asymptotes: $$\phi_A = \frac{(2q + 1)180^\circ}{3} = \mathbf{60^\circ, 180^\circ, 300^\circ} \quad (q = 0, 1, 2)$$ Centroid $\sigma_A$ on the real axis: $$\sigma_A = \frac{\sum \text{Re}(\text{poles}) - \sum \text{Re}(\text{zeros})}{n - m} = \frac{[0 + (+1) + (-2) + (-2)] - (-1)}{3} = \frac{-3 - (-1)}{3} = \mathbf{-\frac{2}{3} \approx -0.667}$$

Step 4: Breakaway & Break-in Points (Rule 6)

From the characteristic equation: $K(s) = -\frac{s^4 + 3s^3 + 12s^2 - 16s}{s + 1}$. $$\frac{dK}{ds} = -\frac{(4s^3 + 9s^2 + 24s - 16)(s + 1) - (s^4 + 3s^3 + 12s^2 - 16s)}{(s + 1)^2} = 0$$ Expanding the numerator: $$3s^4 + 10s^3 + 21s^2 + 24s - 16 = 0$$ Solving for real roots:
• $s_1 = +0.4483$: Lies in the active segment $(0, 1)$. Here the branches from $s=0$ and $s=+1$ collide and break away into the complex plane ($K = 3.12 > 0$). $\implies$ Valid Breakaway Point!
• $s_2 = -2.2627$: Lies in the active segment $(-\infty, -1)$. The complex branches re-enter the real axis at this coordinate ($K = 38.65 > 0$). $\implies$ Valid Break-in Point!

Constructing the Routh array for $q(s) = s^4 + 3s^3 + 12s^2 + (K - 16)s + K = 0$:

Row Col 1 Col 2 Col 3
$s^4$ $1$ $12$ $K$
$s^3$ $3$ $K - 16$ $0$
$s^2$ $\frac{36 - (K - 16)}{3} = \frac{52 - K}{3}$ $K$ $0$
