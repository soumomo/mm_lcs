Archetype • Open-Loop Unstable Plant with RHP Pole

Stability Analysis of $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$ via Nyquist Criterion

“The open loop transfer function of a unity feedback control system is given by $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$ with $K > 0$. Sketch the complete Nyquist plot and determine the range of $K$ for which the closed-loop system is stable. Verify your result using the Routh-Hurwitz criterion.”
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularity Inventory & Closed-Loop Stability Condition

Identify the poles and zeros of the open-loop transfer function:

$$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$$
  • Open-loop zeros: $s = -3$ (lies in the Left-Half Plane, LHP).
  • Open-loop poles: $s = 0$ (on imaginary axis at the origin), $s = +1$ (lies strictly in the open Right-Half Plane, RHP).

Consequently, the number of open-loop poles in the open Right-Half Plane is:

$$P = 1$$

For the closed-loop system to be stable, all roots of the characteristic equation $1 + G(s)H(s) = 0$ must lie strictly in the LHP. Hence, the number of closed-loop poles in the RHP must be:

$$Z = 0$$

According to the Nyquist Stability Criterion (under Prof. Madhubanti Maitra's convention):

$$N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z = 1 - 0 = +1$$

Fundamental Requirement: The closed Nyquist plot of $G(s)H(s)$ must encircle the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ exactly once in the counter-clockwise (CCW) direction for the closed-loop system to be stable.

Step 2: Frequency Response Formulation on Path $C_1$ ($s = j\omega, \omega: 0^+ \to +\infty$)

Substitute $s = j\omega$ into the open-loop transfer function:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K(j\omega + 3)}{j\omega(j\omega - 1)} = \frac{K(3 + j\omega)}{-\omega^2 - j\omega} = \frac{K(3 + j\omega)}{-\omega(\omega + j)}$$

To separate into real and imaginary parts, multiply numerator and denominator by $(-\omega^2 + j\omega)$:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K(3 + j\omega)(-\omega^2 + j\omega)}{\omega^4 + \omega^2} = \frac{K\left[-3\omega^2 + 3j\omega - j\omega^3 - \omega^2\right]}{\omega^2(\omega^2 + 1)}$$ $$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K\left[-4\omega^2 + j\omega(3 - \omega^2)\right]}{\omega^2(\omega^2 + 1)}$$

Dividing through by $\omega^2$ in the real and imaginary components:

$$\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{-4K}{\omega^2 + 1}$$ $$\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{K(3 - \omega^2)}{\omega(\omega^2 + 1)}$$
Step 3: Phase Crossover Frequency & Real-Axis Intercept

The phase crossover frequency $\omega_{pc}$ occurs where the Nyquist locus crosses the real axis, i.e., where $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = 0$:

$$\frac{K(3 - \omega_{pc}^2)}{\omega_{pc}(\omega_{pc}^2 + 1)} = 0 \implies 3 - \omega_{pc}^2 = 0 \implies \omega_{pc} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.7320 \text{ rad/s}$$

Evaluate the real part at this phase crossover frequency:

$$\text{Re}[G(j\sqrt{3})H(j\sqrt{3})] = \frac{-4K}{(\sqrt{3})^2 + 1} = \frac{-4K}{3 + 1} = -K$$

Thus, the polar plot crosses the negative real axis at the coordinate:

$$\mathbf{(-K, 0)}$$
Step 4: Asymptotic Limits of Path $C_1$

Examine the terminal behavior of the polar curve:

  • As $\omega \to 0^+$: $$\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{-4K}{0 + 1} = -4K$$ $$\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \frac{3K}{\omega} = +\infty$$ $$\angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{3}\right) - 90^\circ - (180^\circ - \tan^{-1}\omega) \to 0^\circ - 90^\circ - 180^\circ = -270^\circ \equiv +90^\circ$$ Therefore, at low frequencies, the locus approaches a vertical asymptote at $\text{Re} = -4K$ heading towards $+j\infty$ in the second quadrant.
  • For $0 < \omega < \sqrt{3}$: $\text{Re} < 0$ and $\text{Im} > 0$ (Locus traverses the 2nd quadrant).
  • At $\omega = \sqrt{3}$: Locus crosses the real axis at $(-K, 0)$ heading downward into the 3rd quadrant.
  • As $\omega \to +\infty$: $$|G(j\omega)H(j\omega)| \approx \frac{K\omega}{\omega^2} = \frac{K}{\omega} \to 0$$ $$\lim_{\omega \to +\infty} \angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ = -90^\circ$$ The locus terminates at the origin $(0,0)$ tangent to the negative imaginary axis (entering along $-90^\circ$ in the 3rd quadrant).
Step 5: Mapping the Remaining Segments of the Canonical Contour $\Gamma_s$
  • Path $C_2$ (Infinite Semicircle $s = R e^{j\theta}, R \to \infty, \theta: +90^\circ \to -90^\circ$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K s}{s^2} = \frac{K}{s} = \frac{K}{R} e^{-j\theta} \to 0$$ Maps to the origin $(0,0)$ in the $G(s)H(s)$-plane.
  • Path $C_3$ (Negative imaginary axis $\omega: -\infty \to 0^-$): The mapping is the exact complex conjugate reflection of Path $C_1$ about the real axis. It leaves the origin at $+90^\circ$, traverses the 2nd quadrant, crosses the negative real axis at $(-K, 0)$ at $\omega = -\sqrt{3}$ rad/s, and descends toward the vertical asymptote $\text{Re} = -4K$ with $\text{Im} \to -\infty$.
  • Path $C_4$ (Infinitesimal origin indentation $s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}, \varepsilon \to 0, \theta: -90^\circ \to +90^\circ$): Evaluating the transfer function near $s = 0$: $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K(0 + 3)}{\varepsilon e^{j\theta}(0 - 1)} = \frac{3K}{-\varepsilon e^{j\theta}} = \frac{3K}{\varepsilon} e^{j(180^\circ - \theta)}$$ As $\theta$ sweeps counter-clockwise from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$: The phase angle $(180^\circ - \theta)$ sweeps: $$180^\circ - (-90^\circ) = +270^\circ \longrightarrow 180^\circ \longrightarrow 180^\circ - 90^\circ = +90^\circ$$ With radius $R_\infty = \frac{3K}{\varepsilon} \to \infty$, this forms a vast semicircle of infinite radius in the Left-Half Plane (traversed in the counter-clockwise sense, from $-j\infty$ through $-\infty$ on the real axis to $+j\infty$). This segment joins the terminus of Path $C_3$ at $(-4K - j\infty)$ to the start of Path $C_1$ at $(-4K + j\infty)$.
Figure 3.1: Complete Closed Nyquist Diagram for Open-Loop Unstable System $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$ ($P=1$), highlighting the single CCW encirclement of $(-1+j0)$ when $K > 1$.
Step 6: Encirclement Evaluation & Range of $K$ for Stability

Consider the closed contour formed by $C_1 \cup C_2 \cup C_3 \cup C_4$:

  • The locus crosses the negative real axis at coordinate $(-K, 0)$.
  • The infinite semicircular arc $C_4$ sweeps through the Left-Half Plane from $-j\infty$ around $-\infty$ to $+j\infty$ in the counter-clockwise direction.
  • Along the negative real axis, there are two distinct intervals:
    1. Interval $(-\infty, -K)$: Tracing the boundary $C_3 \to C_4 \to C_1$, points located in this outer interval are encircled in the counter-clockwise direction once ($N_{\text{ccw}} = 1$).
    2. Interval $(-K, 0)$: Points in this interior segment between the origin and $-K$ lie outside the infinite closure, resulting in zero net encirclement ($N_{\text{ccw}} = 0$).

To satisfy the stability criterion $N_{\text{ccw}} = P = 1$, the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ must reside strictly in the region encircled once counter-clockwise, i.e., in the interval $(-\infty, -K)$:

$$-1 < -K \implies \mathbf{K > 1}$$

When $K > 1$:

$$N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 1 = 0 \quad \text{\textbf{(STABLE)}}$$

When $0 < K < 1$:

$$-K > -1 \implies \text{Critical point } (-1+j0) \text{ falls in } (-K, 0) \implies N_{\text{ccw}} = 0$$ $$Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 0 = 1 \quad \text{\textbf{(UNSTABLE, 1 closed-loop pole in RHP)}}$$

When $K = 1$: The Nyquist plot passes directly through $(-1 + j0)$ at $\omega = \sqrt{3}$ rad/s, indicating marginal stability with sustained oscillations.

Step 7: Verification via Routh-Hurwitz Stability Criterion

The closed-loop characteristic equation is:

$$1 + G(s)H(s) = 0 \implies s(s - 1) + K(s + 3) = 0$$ $$s^2 - s + Ks + 3K = 0 \implies s^2 + (K - 1)s + 3K = 0$$

Construct the Routh array:

Row Col 1 Col 2
$s^2$ $1$ $3K$
$s^1$ $K - 1$ $0$
$s^0$ $3K$ $0$

For all roots to lie strictly in the LHP, all elements in the first column must be strictly positive:

  1. $1 > 0$ (satisfied).
  2. $K - 1 > 0 \implies \mathbf{K > 1}$.
  3. $3K > 0 \implies K > 0$.

The Routh-Hurwitz criterion confirms that the system is strictly stable if and only if $K > 1$, in exact agreement with the Nyquist criterion. At $K = 1$, the auxiliary equation yields $s^2 + 3 = 0 \implies s = \pm j\sqrt{3}$, proving that sustained oscillations occur at precisely $\omega_{pc} = \sqrt{3}$ rad/s!

Examiner Pitfall Alert • Class Test Trap Many students lose the full 12 marks by automatically assuming $N = 0$ is required for stability. Remember: $N_{\text{ccw}} = 0$ guarantees stability only for open-loop stable plants ($P = 0$). For plants with open-loop RHP poles ($P \ge 1$), stability demands $N_{\text{ccw}} = P$. Always explicitly state $P = 1 \implies N_{\text{ccw}} = 1$.

Theory • Complex Variable Foundation of Nyquist Criterion

Cauchy's Principle of Argument & Derivation of $N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z$

“State and derive Cauchy's Principle of Argument using complex integration. Explain why an enclosed zero contributes $-2\pi$ radians of net angle while an enclosed pole contributes $+2\pi$ radians when traversed clockwise. Show clearly how this principle establishes the Nyquist Stability Criterion, and explain why the critical point is $(-1+j0)$ rather than the origin.”
Step 1: Statement of Cauchy's Principle of Argument

Let $F(s)$ be a single-valued meromorphic function that is analytic inside and on a closed contour $\Gamma_s$ in the complex $s$-plane, except at a finite number of isolated poles. Assume that no zeros or poles of $F(s)$ lie directly on $\Gamma_s$.

As a point $s$ traverses the contour $\Gamma_s$ once in the clockwise (CW) direction, the mapped locus $\Gamma_F$ in the $F(s)$-plane encircles the origin $N_{\text{cw}}$ times in the clockwise direction, given by:

$$N_{\text{cw}} = Z - P \iff N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z$$

where $Z$ is the total number of zeros of $F(s)$ enclosed inside $\Gamma_s$, and $P$ is the total number of poles of $F(s)$ enclosed inside $\Gamma_s$ (counting multiplicities).

Step 2: Analytical Derivation via Complex Logarithmic Integration

Express $F(s)$ in factored pole-zero form:

$$F(s) = K_0 \frac{\prod_{i=1}^Z (s - z_i)}{\prod_{k=1}^P (s - p_k)}$$

Taking the complex natural logarithm of both sides:

$$\ln F(s) = \ln K_0 + \sum_{i=1}^Z \ln(s - z_i) - \sum_{k=1}^P \ln(s - p_k)$$

Differentiating with respect to the complex variable $s$:

$$\frac{F'(s)}{F(s)} = \sum_{i=1}^Z \frac{1}{s - z_i} - \sum_{k=1}^P \frac{1}{s - p_k}$$

Now integrate both sides along the closed contour $\Gamma_s$ in the clockwise direction:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s \text{ (CW)}} \frac{F'(s)}{F(s)} \, ds = \sum_{i=1}^Z \oint_{\Gamma_s \text{ (CW)}} \frac{ds}{s - z_i} - \sum_{k=1}^P \oint_{\Gamma_s \text{ (CW)}} \frac{ds}{s - p_k}$$

According to Cauchy's Residue Theorem, for any singularity $a$ enclosed inside a contour traversed in the clockwise direction (which has negative orientation with respect to the interior):

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s \text{ (CW)}} \frac{ds}{s - a} = -2\pi j$$

For any point lying outside $\Gamma_s$, the integrand $\frac{1}{s - a}$ is analytic throughout the interior of $\Gamma_s$, so Cauchy's Integral Theorem gives:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s} \frac{ds}{s - a_{\text{outside}}} = 0$$

Substituting these evaluations back into the summation:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s \text{ (CW)}} \frac{F'(s)}{F(s)} \, ds = Z(-2\pi j) - P(-2\pi j) = -2\pi j (Z - P) = +2\pi j (P - Z)$$
Step 3: Geometric Argument & Phase Accumulation

Notice that the differential of $\ln F(s)$ can be partitioned into magnitude and phase components:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s} \frac{F'(s)}{F(s)} \, ds = \oint_{\Gamma_F} \frac{dF}{F} = \oint_{\Gamma_F} d[\ln F] = \oint_{\Gamma_F} d[\ln |F| + j \arg F]$$

Because $|F(s)|$ is a single-valued real scalar, traversing a complete closed loop returns $|F(s)|$ to its starting value, making $\oint d[\ln |F|] \equiv 0$. Therefore:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_F} \frac{dF}{F} = j \Delta [\arg F]_{\Gamma_s} = j (\phi_{\text{final}} - \phi_{\text{initial}})$$

Equating this expression with our previous integration result:

$$j \Delta [\arg F] = 2\pi j (P - Z) \implies \Delta [\arg F] = 2\pi (P - Z)$$

The net counter-clockwise encirclements $N_{\text{ccw}}$ of the origin by the locus $\Gamma_F$ is the total phase angle accumulated divided by $2\pi$:

$$N_{\text{ccw}} = \frac{\Delta [\arg F]}{2\pi} = P - Z$$
Figure 3.2: Geometric Mechanics of Vector Angle Rotation • Enclosed singularities sweep a complete $2\pi$ radians, whereas external singularities sweep equal and opposite angles netting $0^\circ$.
Step 4: Why Zeros Add $-2\pi$ and Poles Add $+2\pi$

Consider the overall phase of $F(s)$:

$$\angle F(s) = \sum_{i=1}^Z \angle(s - z_i) - \sum_{k=1}^P \angle(s - p_k)$$
  • As $s$ traverses $\Gamma_s$ clockwise, every vector $(s - z_i)$ from an enclosed zero rotates by a full circle clockwise: $\Delta \theta_{z_i} = -2\pi$. Since zeros appear in the numerator with a positive sign, their contribution to $\angle F(s)$ is: $$\Delta \phi_{\text{zero}} = +1 \times (-2\pi) = -2\pi$$
  • Every vector $(s - p_k)$ from an enclosed pole also rotates clockwise by $-2\pi$. However, because poles reside in the denominator, they are subtracted in the phase summation: $$\Delta \phi_{\text{pole}} = -1 \times (-2\pi) = +2\pi$$

Hence, the net phase accumulated over the complete clockwise traversal is:

$$\Delta \angle F(s) = Z(-2\pi) - P(-2\pi) = -2\pi(Z - P) = 2\pi(P - Z)$$
Step 5: Transition from $F(s)$-plane to $G(s)H(s)$-plane and the $(-1+j0)$ Critical Point

In feedback control systems, the characteristic equation is:

$$F(s) = 1 + G(s)H(s) = 0 \implies G(s)H(s) = F(s) - 1$$
  • Poles of $F(s)$ vs $G(s)H(s)$: Since $F(s) = 1 + G(s)H(s)$, any pole of $G(s)H(s)$ is identically a pole of $F(s)$. Thus, $P$ is the number of open-loop poles in the RHP.
  • Zeros of $F(s)$ vs Closed-Loop Poles: The zeros of $F(s)$ are the solutions to $1 + G(s)H(s) = 0$. These are the poles of the closed-loop transfer function $T(s) = \frac{G(s)}{1 + G(s)H(s)}$. Thus, $Z$ is the number of closed-loop poles in the RHP.
  • Mapping Equivalence: Notice that $G(s)H(s) = F(s) - 1$. The origin $(0 + j0)$ in the $F(s)$-plane corresponds to: $$F(s) = 0 \iff G(s)H(s) = -1 + j0$$ Plotting $G(s)H(s)$ directly eliminates the need to calculate $1 + G(s)H(s)$ at every frequency. Encirclement of the origin by $1 + G(s)H(s)$ is topologically identical to encirclement of the point $(-1 + j0)$ by the open-loop transfer function locus $G(s)H(s)$!

Benchmark • Complete Nyquist Mapping of Canonical Contour

Complete Nyquist Plot for Type 1 Third-Order System: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(1+T_1 s)(1+T_2 s)}$

“Sketch the complete Nyquist plot for a system having $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(1+T_1 s)(1+T_2 s)}$. Detail the mapping of all 4 paths of the Nyquist contour. Determine the phase crossover frequency $\omega_{pc}$, the real-axis intercept, and find the condition on $K$ for closed-loop stability. Illustrate specifically for $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+1)(s+2)}$.”
Step 1: The Canonical Nyquist Contour $\Gamma_s$

To detect all possible closed-loop poles in the unstable right-half plane, the contour $\Gamma_s$ must encompass the entire open Right-Half Plane ($\text{Re}(s) > 0$). Because the plant has a pole at the origin $s = 0$, the contour must be indented into the right-half plane by an infinitesimal semicircle of radius $\varepsilon \to 0$.

The canonical contour $\Gamma_s$ consists of 4 distinct segments:

  1. Path $C_1$: The positive imaginary axis from $s = +j\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon \to 0$) to $s = +jR$ ($R \to \infty$).
  2. Path $C_2$: Semicircle of infinite radius $s = R e^{j\theta}$ with $R \to \infty$, where $\theta$ sweeps from $+90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to -90^\circ$ (CW).
  3. Path $C_3$: The negative imaginary axis from $s = -jR$ ($R \to \infty$) to $s = -j\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon \to 0$).
  4. Path $C_4$: Infinitesimal semicircular indentation around the origin $s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}$ with $\varepsilon \to 0$, where $\theta$ sweeps from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$ (CCW in $s$-plane).
Figure 3.3: Canonical Closed Nyquist Contour $\Gamma_s$ in the complex $s$-plane enclosing the entire Right-Half Plane with origin indentation.
Step 2: Mathematical Mapping of the 4 Contour Paths
  • Path $C_1$ ($s = j\omega, \omega \in (0^+, +\infty)$): $$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K}{j\omega(1 + j\omega T_1)(1 + j\omega T_2)}$$ Magnitude and phase: $$|G(j\omega)H(j\omega)| = \frac{K}{\omega \sqrt{1 + \omega^2 T_1^2}\sqrt{1 + \omega^2 T_2^2}}$$ $$\phi(\omega) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(\omega T_1) - \tan^{-1}(\omega T_2)$$ As $\omega \to 0^+$: $|GH| \to \infty$ and $\phi \to -90^\circ$. The real part at low frequencies approaches: $$\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = -K(T_1 + T_2)$$ As $\omega \to +\infty$: $|GH| \to 0$ and $\phi \to -90^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ = -270^\circ \equiv +90^\circ$.
  • Path $C_2$ ($s = R e^{j\theta}, R \to \infty, \theta: +90^\circ \to -90^\circ$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K}{(R e^{j\theta})(T_1 R e^{j\theta})(T_2 R e^{j\theta})} = \frac{K}{T_1 T_2 R^3} e^{-j3\theta} \to 0$$ Because the number of poles $n = 3$ exceeds the number of zeros $m = 0$, Path $C_2$ collapses to the origin $(0,0)$.
  • Path $C_3$ ($s = j\omega, \omega \in (-\infty, 0^-)$): This is the complex conjugate of Path $C_1$, reflected across the real axis. It leaves the origin at $-90^\circ$ ($-270^\circ$), crosses the negative real axis at $-\frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2}$, and terminates at $-K(T_1 + T_2) + j\infty$.
  • Path $C_4$ ($s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}, \varepsilon \to 0, \theta: -90^\circ \to +90^\circ$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K}{\varepsilon e^{j\theta}(1 + 0)(1 + 0)} = \frac{K}{\varepsilon} e^{-j\theta} = R_\infty e^{-j\theta}$$ As $\theta$ sweeps counter-clockwise from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$: The phase $-\theta$ sweeps clockwise: $$-(-90^\circ) = +90^\circ \longrightarrow 0^\circ \longrightarrow -90^\circ$$ This forms a huge semicircle of infinite radius spanning the entire Right-Half Plane in the clockwise direction from $+j\infty$ through $+\infty$ on the real axis to $-j\infty$.
Step 3: Analytical Derivation of Phase Crossover & Real Intercept

At the phase crossover frequency $\omega_{pc}$, the phase angle is $-180^\circ$:

$$\phi(\omega_{pc}) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_1) - \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_2) = -180^\circ$$ $$\tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_1) + \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_2) = 90^\circ$$

Taking the tangent of both sides:

$$\tan\left[\tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_1) + \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_2)\right] = \frac{\omega_{pc}(T_1 + T_2)}{1 - \omega_{pc}^2 T_1 T_2} = \tan(90^\circ) = \infty$$

For the fraction to become infinite, its denominator must vanish:

$$1 - \omega_{pc}^2 T_1 T_2 = 0 \implies \mathbf{\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}}$$

Substitute $\omega_{pc}$ into the magnitude expression:

$$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{K}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{T_1^2}{T_1 T_2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{T_2^2}{T_1 T_2}}} = \frac{K \sqrt{T_1 T_2}}{\sqrt{\frac{T_2 + T_1}{T_2}} \sqrt{\frac{T_1 + T_2}{T_1}}}$$ $$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{K \sqrt{T_1 T_2}}{\frac{T_1 + T_2}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}} = \mathbf{\frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2}}$$

The Nyquist locus crosses the negative real axis at coordinate:

$$\left(-\frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2}, 0\right)$$
Step 4: Application to JU Benchmark Plant $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+1)(s+2)}$

Express $G(s)H(s)$ in standard time-constant form:

$$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+1) \cdot 2(1 + 0.5s)} = \frac{K/2}{s(1 + s)(1 + 0.5s)}$$

Here, $K' = K/2$, $T_1 = 1\text{ s}$, and $T_2 = 0.5\text{ s}$.

  • Phase Crossover Frequency: $$\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 \times 0.5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.5}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142\text{ rad/s}$$
  • Real-Axis Intercept: $$|G(j\sqrt{2})H(j\sqrt{2})| = \frac{(K/2)(1)(0.5)}{1 + 0.5} = \frac{K/4}{1.5} = \frac{K}{6}$$ The locus crosses the negative real axis at $\mathbf{-\frac{K}{6}}$.
  • Low-Frequency Asymptote: $$\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = -K'(T_1 + T_2) = -\frac{K}{2}(1 + 0.5) = -0.75K$$
Figure 3.4: Complete Nyquist Plot for Type 1 System $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+1)(s+2)}$, showing stability for $K < 6$, marginal stability at $K = 6$, and instability for $K > 6$.
Step 5: Stability Determination & Critical Gain $K_{\text{mar}}$

Open-loop poles of $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+1)(s+2)}$ are $s = 0, -1, -2$. Hence:

$$P = 0$$

For closed-loop stability, we require $Z = 0 \implies N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z = 0$. That is, the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ must not be encircled by the Nyquist plot.

  • Case 1 ($K < 6$): $$-\frac{K}{6} > -1 \iff \frac{K}{6} < 1 \implies K < 6$$ The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ lies to the left of the negative real-axis intercept $-\frac{K}{6}$. It remains entirely outside the closed Nyquist loop: $$N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 - 0 = 0 \quad \text{\textbf{(STABLE)}}$$
  • Case 2 ($K = 6$): $$-\frac{K}{6} = -1 \implies K_{\text{mar}} = 6$$ The Nyquist plot passes directly through $(-1 + j0)$ at $\omega = \sqrt{2}$ rad/s. The closed-loop system is marginally stable, exhibiting sustained sinusoidal oscillations at frequency $\omega = \sqrt{2}\text{ rad/s}$.
  • Case 3 ($K > 6$): $$-\frac{K}{6} < -1 \implies K > 6$$ The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ falls inside the loop formed by $C_1, C_3,$ and $C_4$. Tracing the loop reveals that $(-1 + j0)$ is encircled twice in the clockwise direction: $$N_{\text{cw}} = 2 \iff N_{\text{ccw}} = -2$$ $$Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 - (-2) = 2 \quad \text{\textbf{(UNSTABLE, 2 closed-loop poles in RHP)}}$$

Conclusion: The system is closed-loop stable for the gain range:

$$\mathbf{0 < K < 6}$$
Analytical Verification via Routh Array Characteristic equation: $s(s+1)(s+2) + K = s^3 + 3s^2 + 2s + K = 0$. The $s^1$ row in the Routh array is $\frac{3(2) - K}{3} = \frac{6 - K}{3}$. For stability: $6 - K > 0 \implies K < 6$. Marginal stability occurs at $K = 6$, giving the auxiliary equation $3s^2 + 6 = 0 \implies s = \pm j\sqrt{2}$ rad/s, perfectly corroborating $\omega_{pc} = \sqrt{2}$.

Pathology • Double Pole at Origin & 360° Enclosure

Type 2 System with Double Pole at Origin: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s^2(s+1)}$

“Draw the complete Nyquist plot of a unity feedback system whose open-loop transfer function is $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s^2(s+1)}$ with $K > 0$. Pay specific attention to the mapping of the indentation around the double pole at the origin. Evaluate the encirclement of $(-1+j0)$ and comment on the closed-loop stability.”
Step 1: Open-Loop Poles & Frequency Response on Path $C_1$

The open-loop poles are $s = 0, 0$ (double pole at the origin) and $s = -1$ (LHP).

$$P = 0$$

Evaluate along Path $C_1$ ($s = j\omega, \omega \in (0^+, +\infty)$):

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K}{(j\omega)^2(1 + j\omega)} = \frac{K}{-\omega^2(1 + j\omega)} = \frac{-K(1 - j\omega)}{\omega^2(1 + \omega^2)}$$ $$\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{-K}{\omega^2(1 + \omega^2)}$$ $$\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{K}{\omega(1 + \omega^2)}$$

Critical Observation:

  • For all finite $\omega > 0$, $\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] < 0$ and $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] > 0$.
  • Therefore, Path $C_1$ lies strictly and entirely in the second quadrant!
  • Because $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] \ne 0$ for any finite $\omega > 0$, the polar plot never intersects the negative real axis at any finite non-zero frequency!
  • As $\omega \to 0^+$: $|GH| \to \infty, \phi \to -180^\circ$. The locus is asymptotic to $-\infty + j\infty$.
  • As $\omega \to +\infty$: $|GH| \to 0, \phi \to -180^\circ - 90^\circ = -270^\circ \equiv +90^\circ$. Approaches the origin tangent to the $+j$ axis.
Step 2: Mapping of Path $C_4$ (Double Pole Indentation)

To bypass the double pole at $s = 0$, the contour $\Gamma_s$ takes an infinitesimal semicircular indentation $s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}$ with $\varepsilon \to 0$, where $\theta$ sweeps from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$ ($\Delta \theta = +180^\circ$ counter-clockwise in the $s$-plane).

Substitute into the transfer function near $s = 0$:

$$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K}{(\varepsilon e^{j\theta})^2(0 + 1)} = \frac{K}{\varepsilon^2 e^{j2\theta}} = \frac{K}{\varepsilon^2} e^{-j2\theta} = R_\infty e^{-j2\theta}$$

As $\theta$ sweeps from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$:

  • At $\theta = -90^\circ$: $\phi = -2(-90^\circ) = +180^\circ$ (negative real axis).
  • At $\theta = -45^\circ$: $\phi = -2(-45^\circ) = +90^\circ$ (positive imaginary axis).
  • At $\theta = 0^\circ$: $\phi = 0^\circ$ (positive real axis).
  • At $\theta = +45^\circ$: $\phi = -90^\circ$ (negative imaginary axis).
  • At $\theta = +90^\circ$: $\phi = -2(+90^\circ) = -180^\circ$ (negative real axis).

Total phase angle swept:

$$\Delta \phi = -180^\circ - (+180^\circ) = \mathbf{-360^\circ}$$

Key Result: The indentation around the double pole at the origin maps to one complete circular revolution of infinite radius ($360^\circ$) traversed in the clockwise direction! This vast circular closure connects Path $C_3$ (which ends at $\omega = 0^-$ with phase $+180^\circ$) to Path $C_1$ (which starts at $\omega = 0^+$ with phase $-180^\circ$).

Figure 3.5: Nyquist Plot for Type 2 System $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s^2(s+1)}$ with double pole at origin, showing 2 clockwise encirclements of $(-1+j0)$ and unconditional instability.
Step 3: Encirclement Evaluation & Closed-Loop Instability

Inspect the critical point $(-1 + j0)$:

  • Because Path $C_1$ lies in Quadrant II and Path $C_3$ lies in Quadrant III, the polar plot does not intersect the negative real axis anywhere between $0$ and $-\infty$.
  • The infinite circular arc $C_4$ starts on the negative real axis at $-\infty$, sweeps clockwise across the entire right-half plane, and closes back onto the negative real axis.
  • Thus, the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ is enclosed within the $360^\circ$ loop and is encircled twice in the clockwise direction: $$N_{\text{cw}} = 2 \iff N_{\text{ccw}} = -2$$

Evaluate the number of unstable closed-loop poles $Z$:

$$Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 - (-2) = \mathbf{2}$$

Conclusion: For every value of $K > 0$, the closed-loop system possesses two roots in the right-half $s$-plane. The uncompensated Type 2 third-order system is unconditionally unstable!

Routh-Hurwitz Cross-Check: Characteristic equation is $s^3 + s^2 + K = 0$. Routh array:

RowCol 1Col 2
$s^3$$1$$0$
$s^2$$1$$K$
$s^1$$-K$$0$
$s^0$$K$$0$

For any $K > 0$, the signs of the first column are $+1, +1, -K, +K$ (exactly two sign changes), proving $Z = 2$ unstable poles in strict accordance with Nyquist.

Design Metrics • Gain Margin, Phase Margin & Relative Stability

Analytical Computation of Gain Margin (GM) and Phase Margin (PM)

“Define Gain Margin and Phase Margin from the polar plot. Provide their analytical mathematical formulas. Calculate the exact Gain Margin and Phase Margin for:
(a) $G(s)H(s) = \frac{10}{s(1+0.1s)(1+0.05s)}$ [JU 2024 Supp Q5(iii)]
(b) $G(s)H(s) = \frac{2}{s(s+1)(s+2)}$ [JU 2018 Q6(i)].”
Step 1: Rigorous Definitions & Analytical Formulas
  • Phase Crossover Frequency ($\omega_{pc}$): The frequency at which the open-loop phase angle is $-180^\circ$: $$\angle G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ \iff \text{Im}[G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})] = 0$$
  • Gain Margin ($GM$): The reciprocal of the open-loop magnitude at the phase crossover frequency. It represents the factor by which the system gain can be increased before the system becomes unstable: $$K_g = \frac{1}{|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|}$$ $$GM_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(K_g) = -20 \log_{10} |G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|$$
  • Gain Crossover Frequency ($\omega_{gc}$): The frequency at which the open-loop magnitude is unity ($1.0$ or $0\text{ dB}$): $$|G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})| = 1.0$$
  • Phase Margin ($PM$): The additional phase lag required at the gain crossover frequency to bring the system to the verge of instability: $$PM = 180^\circ + \angle G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})$$
Figure 3.6: Geometric representation of Gain Margin ($GM = 1/a$) and Phase Margin ($PM = 180^\circ + \phi_{gc}$) on the Nyquist Polar Plot.
Step 2: Solved Benchmark 1 • JU 2024 Supplementary Q5(iii)

Plant:

$$G(s)H(s) = \frac{10}{s(1 + 0.1s)(1 + 0.05s)}$$

Here, $K = 10$, $T_1 = 0.1\text{ s}$, and $T_2 = 0.05\text{ s}$.

  1. Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_{pc}$: $$\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.1 \times 0.05}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{0.005}} = \frac{1}{0.07071} = \mathbf{14.142\text{ rad/s}}$$
  2. Magnitude at $\omega_{pc}$: $$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2} = \frac{10 \times 0.1 \times 0.05}{0.1 + 0.05} = \frac{0.05}{0.15} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333$$
  3. Gain Margin ($GM$): $$K_g = \frac{1}{a} = \frac{1}{1/3} = 3.0$$ $$GM_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(3.0) = \mathbf{+9.542\text{ dB}}$$
  4. Gain Crossover Frequency $\omega_{gc}$: Set magnitude equal to unity: $$|G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})| = \frac{10}{\omega_{gc} \sqrt{1 + (0.1\omega_{gc})^2}\sqrt{1 + (0.05\omega_{gc})^2}} = 1.0$$ Squaring both sides: $$\omega_{gc}^2 (1 + 0.01\omega_{gc}^2)(1 + 0.0025\omega_{gc}^2) = 100$$ Solving the polynomial yields: $$\mathbf{\omega_{gc} \approx 7.55\text{ rad/s}}$$
  5. Phase at $\omega_{gc}$: $$\phi(\omega_{gc}) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(0.1 \times 7.55) - \tan^{-1}(0.05 \times 7.55)$$ $$\phi(\omega_{gc}) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(0.755) - \tan^{-1}(0.3775) = -90^\circ - 37.05^\circ - 20.68^\circ = -147.73^\circ$$
  6. Phase Margin ($PM$): $$PM = 180^\circ + (-147.73^\circ) = \mathbf{+32.27^\circ}$$

Stability Assessment: Because $\omega_{gc} (7.55\text{ rad/s}) < \omega_{pc} (14.14\text{ rad/s})$, $GM = +9.54\text{ dB} > 0$, and $PM = +32.27^\circ > 0$, the closed-loop system is stable with robust relative stability margins.

Step 3: Solved Benchmark 2 • JU 2018 Q6(i)

Plant:

$$G(s)H(s) = \frac{2}{s(s+1)(s+2)} = \frac{1}{s(1+s)(1+0.5s)}$$

Here, $K' = 1$, $T_1 = 1\text{ s}$, and $T_2 = 0.5\text{ s}$.

  1. Phase Crossover Frequency: $$\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 \times 0.5}} = \sqrt{2} \approx \mathbf{1.414\text{ rad/s}}$$
  2. Magnitude at $\omega_{pc}$: $$|G(j\sqrt{2})H(j\sqrt{2})| = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{1+2} \cdot \sqrt{4+2}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{6}} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$$
  3. Gain Margin: $$GM = \frac{1}{1/3} = 3.0 \implies GM_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(3) = \mathbf{+9.542\text{ dB}}$$
  4. Gain Crossover Frequency: $$\frac{2}{\omega_{gc} \sqrt{\omega_{gc}^2 + 1} \sqrt{\omega_{gc}^2 + 4}} = 1.0 \implies \mathbf{\omega_{gc} \approx 0.752\text{ rad/s}}$$
  5. Phase Margin: $$\phi(\omega_{gc}) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(0.752) - \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{0.752}{2}\right) = -90^\circ - 36.94^\circ - 20.61^\circ = -147.55^\circ$$ $$PM = 180^\circ - 147.55^\circ = \mathbf{+32.45^\circ}$$
Relative Stability Criteria Summary
  • Stable: $\omega_{gc} < \omega_{pc} \iff GM_{\text{dB}} > 0\text{ dB}$ and $PM > 0^\circ$.
  • Marginally Stable: $\omega_{gc} = \omega_{pc} \iff GM_{\text{dB}} = 0\text{ dB}$ and $PM = 0^\circ$.
  • Unstable: $\omega_{gc} > \omega_{pc} \iff GM_{\text{dB}} < 0\text{ dB}$ and $PM < 0^\circ$.
