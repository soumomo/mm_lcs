Section 3.1 Theoretical Foundations • Complex Variable Mapping

Complex Variable Foundations & Cauchy's Principle of the Argument

The Nyquist Stability Criterion, formulated by Swedish-American engineer Harry Nyquist at Bell Laboratories in 1932, solves one of the most profound dilemmas of control engineering: How to guarantee the stability of a closed-loop system using only the open-loop frequency response, without ever factoring the characteristic equation or needing an explicit mathematical model.

01

Why Frequency Domain?

While Routh-Hurwitz requires an exact analytical transfer function and offers binary (YES/NO) stability without any safety margins, the Nyquist technique directly accommodates experimental sinusoidal test data, non-rational transport lags ($e^{-s\tau}$), and quantifies exact robustness margins ($GM$, $PM$).

02

Conformal Mapping

A continuous, closed contour $\Gamma_s$ in the complex $s$-plane is mapped through an analytic function $F(s)$ into a corresponding closed contour $\Gamma_F$ in the $F(s)$-plane. Under conformal mapping, angle magnitudes and orientation directions between intersecting curves are strictly preserved.

03

Origin Shift to (-1 + j0)

Because $F(s) = 1 + G(s)H(s)$, evaluating $G(s)H(s) = F(s) - 1$ is equivalent to shifting the reference frame horizontally by 1 unit to the left. Encirclement of the origin in the $F(s)$-plane is identical to encirclement of the critical point $-1 + j0$ in the $G(s)H(s)$-plane.

Fundamental Theorem • Cauchy's Principle of the Argument
JU Exam Master Theorem

Let $F(s)$ be a single-valued, meromorphic function with $Z$ zeros and $P$ poles inside a closed, continuous contour $\Gamma_s$ in the $s$-plane. Assume that $\Gamma_s$ does not pass through any zeros or poles of $F(s)$.

$$N_{\text{cw}} = Z - P \quad \iff \quad N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z$$

Where:
• $N_{\text{cw}}$ is the number of clockwise encirclements of the origin in the $F(s)$-plane (or of $-1 + j0$ in the $G(s)H(s)$-plane) as $s$ traverses $\Gamma_s$ in the clockwise direction.
• $N_{\text{ccw}}$ is the number of counter-clockwise encirclements ($N_{\text{ccw}} = -N_{\text{cw}}$).
• $Z$ is the number of zeros of $F(s) = 1 + G(s)H(s)$ enclosed by $\Gamma_s$ (which are the closed-loop poles).
• $P$ is the number of poles of $F(s)$ enclosed by $\Gamma_s$ (which are the open-loop poles of $G(s)H(s)$).

Mapping from $s$-plane through the characteristic $F(s)$-plane to the physical $G(s)H(s)$-plane

Vector Architecture
A

Proof 1: Complex Logarithmic Integration

Consider the contour integral of the logarithmic derivative of $F(s)$ along $\Gamma_s$:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s} \frac{F'(s)}{F(s)} \, ds = \oint_{\Gamma_s} d[\ln F(s)] = \oint_{\Gamma_s} d[\ln |F(s)| + j \theta_F]$$

Since $\Gamma_s$ is a closed curve, $\ln |F(s)|$ begins and ends at the exact same value ($\Delta \ln |F(s)| = 0$). Hence, the integral reduces purely to the imaginary net phase variation:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s} \frac{F'(s)}{F(s)} \, ds = j \Delta \theta_F$$

By Cauchy's Residue Theorem, each interior zero $z_i$ of multiplicity $m_i$ creates a simple pole of $F'(s)/F(s)$ with residue $+m_i$. Each interior pole $p_k$ of multiplicity $n_k$ creates a simple pole with residue $-n_k$:

$$\oint_{\Gamma_s} \frac{F'(s)}{F(s)} \, ds = 2\pi j \left( \sum m_i - \sum n_k \right) = 2\pi j (Z - P)$$

Equating both expressions yields: $j \Delta \theta_F = 2\pi j (Z - P) \implies \Delta \theta_F = 2\pi (Z - P)$. Traversing $\Gamma_s$ clockwise gives a net clockwise phase shift, proving:

$$N_{\text{cw}} = Z - P \quad \text{and} \quad N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z$$
B

Proof 2: Geometric Vector Angle Summation

Factor the characteristic function in zero-pole form:

$$F(s) = K \frac{\prod_{i=1}^Z (s - z_i)}{\prod_{k=1}^P (s - p_k)}$$

The argument (phase angle) of $F(s)$ is the algebraic sum of the individual vector angles:

$$\angle F(s) = \sum_{i=1}^Z \angle(s - z_i) - \sum_{k=1}^P \angle(s - p_k)$$

Let a point $s$ traverse the closed contour $\Gamma_s$ once in the clockwise direction:
• If a zero $z_i$ lies inside $\Gamma_s$, the vector $(s - z_i)$ rotates by $-2\pi$ ($-360^\circ$ clockwise).
• If a zero lies outside $\Gamma_s$, the net angle swept by $(s - z_i)$ as $s$ returns to its starting point is $0^\circ$.
• If a pole $p_k$ lies inside $\Gamma_s$, $(s - p_k)$ rotates by $-2\pi$. Since it appears with a negative sign, its net phase contribution is $-(-2\pi) = +2\pi$.

Therefore, the net angle accumulated by $F(s)$ over the complete loop is:

$$\Delta \theta_F = Z(-2\pi) - P(-2\pi) = -2\pi(Z - P)$$

Dividing by $-2\pi$ gives exactly $N_{\text{cw}} = Z - P$ clockwise encirclements (or $N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z$ counter-clockwise encirclements)!

Section 3.2 Geometric Contour Mapping • 4 Canonical Paths

The Standard Nyquist Contour ($\Gamma_s$) and the 4 Canonical Segments

To ascertain closed-loop stability, the chosen $s$-plane contour $\Gamma_s$ must completely enclose the entire unstable territory: the open Right Half of the complex $s$-plane ($\text{Re}(s) > 0$). To prevent the contour from passing directly through singularities on the imaginary axis, infinitesimal semicircular indentations are carved out into the Right-Half Plane.

Figure 3.3: The Canonical Closed Nyquist Contour $\Gamma_s$ in the $s$-Plane

The 4 distinct mathematical paths encompassing the entire Right-Half Plane with origin indentation

Closed Loop $\Gamma_s$
Path C1

Positive Imaginary Axis ($s = j\omega$)

$\omega: 0^+ \to +\infty$

Substituting $s = j\omega$ into $G(s)H(s)$ produces the conventional frequency response polar plot:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = |G(j\omega)H(j\omega)| e^{j \angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega)}$$

As $\omega$ sweeps from $0^+$ to $+\infty$, the polar locus traverses the quadrants, passing through the phase crossover frequency $\omega_{pc}$ where $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = 0$.

Path C2

Infinite Semicircular Arc ($R \to \infty$)

$\theta: +90^\circ \to -90^\circ$

Let $s = R e^{j\theta}$ where $R \to \infty$ and $\theta$ sweeps clockwise from $+90^\circ$ through $0^\circ$ to $-90^\circ$.

$$\lim_{R \to \infty} G(s)H(s) = \lim_{R \to \infty} \frac{K R^m e^{j m \theta}}{R^n e^{j n \theta}} = \lim_{R \to \infty} \frac{K}{R^{n-m}} e^{-j(n-m)\theta} = 0 \quad (\text{for } n > m)$$

For all physically realizable strictly proper systems ($n > m$), Path $C_2$ collapses into a single stationary point at the origin $(0, 0)$ of the $GH$-plane.

Path C3

Negative Imaginary Axis (Conjugate Mirror)

$\omega: -\infty \to 0^-$

Along Path $C_3$, $s = -j\omega'$ where $\omega' > 0$. Because real physical transfer functions have real polynomial coefficients:

$$G(-j\omega)H(-j\omega) = [G(j\omega)H(j\omega)]^*$$

$|G(-j\omega)H(-j\omega)| = |G(j\omega)H(j\omega)|$ and $\angle G(-j\omega)H(-j\omega) = -\angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega)$. Hence, Path $C_3$ is the exact mirror reflection across the real axis of Path $C_1$.

Path C4

Infinitesimal Semicircular Detours ($\varepsilon \to 0$)

$\theta: -90^\circ \to +90^\circ$

When open-loop poles lie on the $j\omega$-axis (at $s=0$ or $\pm j\omega_0$), we indent the contour around them into the RHP: $s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}$.

$$\lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \frac{K}{s^N} = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \frac{K}{\varepsilon^N e^{j N \theta}} = \infty \cdot e^{-j N \theta}$$

For Type $N$, a clockwise sweep of $180^\circ$ in the $s$-plane maps to an arc of infinite radius sweeping $N \times 180^\circ$ clockwise in the $GH$-plane!

Section 3.3 Sketching Protocol • Gain & Phase Margins

Step-by-Step Construction Procedure & Frequency-Domain Stability Margins

Drawing a complete Nyquist plot by hand requires a systematic algorithmic protocol. In addition to determining binary (YES/NO) absolute stability, the frequency response curve reveals quantitative indices of relative stability: the Gain Margin ($GM$) and Phase Margin ($PM$).

7 The 7-Step Algorithmic Protocol for Constructing Nyquist Plots

Exam Gold Standard
Step 01

Identify Poles & Count $P$

Find the roots of open-loop denominator $D(s) = 0$. Count $P$, the number of poles strictly in the open Right-Half Plane ($\text{Re}(p_k) > 0$). Note any poles on the $j\omega$-axis (at $s = 0$ or $\pm j\omega_0$).

Step 02

Frequency Response Expressions

Substitute $s = j\omega$. Formulate analytical expressions for magnitude $|G(j\omega)H(j\omega)|$ and total phase angle $\angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \sum \angle(j\omega - z_i) - \sum \angle(j\omega - p_k)$.

Step 03

Asymptotic Limits ($\omega \to 0^+, \infty$)

Compute starting point ($\omega \to 0^+$: magnitude $\infty$ or $K_{dc}$, angle $-N \times 90^\circ$) and termination point ($\omega \to \infty$: magnitude $0$, angle $-(n-m) \times 90^\circ$).

Step 04

Phase Crossover ($\omega_{pc}$)

Set $\angle G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ$ (or $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = 0$). Solve for real $\omega_{pc} > 0$. Compute real intercept $a = |G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|$. Crossing point is $(-a, 0)$.

Step 05

Gain Crossover ($\omega_{gc}$)

Set $|G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})| = 1.0$ (unit circle intersection). Solve for $\omega_{gc} > 0$. Compute $\phi_{gc} = \angle G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})$. Phase Margin is $PM = 180^\circ + \phi_{gc}$.

Step 06 & 07

Closure, $N$ Count & Stability

Reflect Path $C_1$ across real axis to obtain $C_3$. Close origin with infinite arc ($C_4$). Count CCW encirclements $N_{\text{ccw}}$ of $(-1 + j0)$. Calculate $Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}}$. Stable iff $Z = 0$!

Gain Margin ($GM$) Analytical Formulation

The Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_{pc}$ is defined as the frequency where the open-loop phase angle is $-180^\circ$:

$$\angle G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ \iff \text{Im}[G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})] = 0$$

Let $a = |G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|$. The Gain Margin represents how much the system gain can be multiplied before the polar curve expands to touch $(-1 + j0)$:

$$GM = \frac{1}{a} = \frac{1}{|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|}$$ $$GM_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(GM) = -20 \log_{10} a$$

• If $a < 1 \implies GM > 1 \implies GM_{\text{dB}} > 0\text{ dB}$ (Stable).
• If $a = 1 \implies GM = 1 \implies GM_{\text{dB}} = 0\text{ dB}$ (Marginal).
• If $a > 1 \implies GM < 1 \implies GM_{\text{dB}} < 0\text{ dB}$ (Unstable).

Phase Margin ($PM$) Analytical Formulation

The Gain Crossover Frequency $\omega_{gc}$ is defined as the frequency where the open-loop magnitude equals unity:

$$|G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})| = 1.0 \quad (0\text{ dB})$$

The Phase Margin is the amount of additional phase lag that can be tolerated at $\omega_{gc}$ before reaching $-180^\circ$:

$$PM = 180^\circ + \angle G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc}) = 180^\circ + \phi_{gc}$$

• If $\phi_{gc} > -180^\circ \implies PM > 0^\circ$ (Positive Margin, Stable).
• If $\phi_{gc} = -180^\circ \implies PM = 0^\circ$ (Marginally Stable).
• If $\phi_{gc} < -180^\circ \implies PM < 0^\circ$ (Negative Margin, Unstable).

Section 3.4 Exhaustive Archetype Analysis • 5 Case Studies

Comprehensive Archetype Case Studies (Deep-Dive Analysis)

Mastering the Nyquist stability criterion requires analyzing canonical archetypes spanning every system type, order, open-loop stability status, and peculiar behaviors such as conditional stability.

Archetype 1

Type 0 Third-Order System: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{(s+1)(s+2)(s+3)}$

P = 0 • No Detour at Origin

1. Poles & Origin Detour: Poles are at $s = -1, -2, -3$ (all in LHP, hence $P = 0$). Because there is no pole at the origin, no indentation is needed ($C_4$ does not exist). The frequency response starts at a finite point on the positive real axis:

$$G(0)H(0) = \frac{K}{1 \times 2 \times 3} = \frac{K}{6} \angle 0^\circ$$

2. High-Frequency Limit ($\omega \to \infty$): With 3 poles and 0 zeros ($n-m=3$), the terminal angle is $-3 \times 90^\circ = -270^\circ$, approaching the origin asymptotically along the positive imaginary axis.

3. Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_{pc}$:

$$\angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = -\tan^{-1}\omega - \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right) - \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega}{3}\right) = -180^\circ$$ $$\tan(A+B+C) = \frac{S_1 - S_3}{1 - S_2} = 0 \implies S_1 = S_3$$ $$\omega + \frac{\omega}{2} + \frac{\omega}{3} = \omega \cdot \frac{\omega}{2} \cdot \frac{\omega}{3} \implies \frac{11}{6}\omega = \frac{\omega^3}{6} \implies \omega_{pc} = \sqrt{11} \approx 3.317\text{ rad/s}$$

4. Intercept on Negative Real Axis:

$$|G(j\sqrt{11})H(j\sqrt{11})| = \frac{K}{\sqrt{1+11}\sqrt{4+11}\sqrt{9+11}} = \frac{K}{\sqrt{12 \times 15 \times 20}} = \frac{K}{60}$$

The polar plot crosses the negative real axis at $\left(-\frac{K}{60}, \; 0\right)$.

5. Stability Range via Nyquist Encirclement:

  • $K < 60$: Crossing is inside $-1$ (i.e. $-\frac{K}{60} > -1$). The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ is NOT encircled ($N = 0$). Since $P = 0$, $Z = 0 - 0 = 0$ → STABLE.
  • $K = 60$: Plot passes directly through $(-1 + j0)$. Marginally stable with sustained oscillations at $\omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{11}$ rad/s.
  • $K > 60$: Plot crosses to the left of $-1$. Critical point is encircled twice clockwise ($N_{\text{cw}} = 2 \implies Z = 2$) → UNSTABLE.

Routh-Hurwitz Verification: Characteristic equation $s^3 + 6s^2 + 11s + (6+K) = 0$. Routh array requires $6 \times 11 - (6+K) > 0 \implies K < 60$. Exact match!

Archetype 2

Type 1 Third-Order System: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(s+1)(s+2)}$ (JU 2018, 2017)

P = 0 • 180° CW Detour

Key Quantitative Metrics:

  • Phase Crossover Frequency: $\omega_{pc} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$ rad/s.
  • Crossing Point: $\left( -\frac{K}{6}, \; 0 \right)$.
  • Vertical Asymptote ($\omega \to 0^+$): $\sigma = -\frac{3K}{4} = -0.75K$.
  • Critical Gain: $K_{\text{crit}} = 6$.

Stability Decision Matrix:

  • $0 < K < 6$: $N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies Z = 0$ (STABLE).
  • $K = 6$: Marginally stable, $\omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{2}$ rad/s.
  • $K > 6$: $N_{\text{cw}} = 2 \implies Z = 2$ (UNSTABLE, 2 RHP poles).
Archetype 3

Type 2 System: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s^2(s+1)}$ (JU 2019, 2024)

P = 0 • Unconditionally Unstable
Type 2 Third-Order: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s^2(s+1)}$ (P = 0)
$N_{\text{cw}} = 2$ $Z = P + N_{\text{cw}} = 2$ Unconditionally Unstable

Profound Insight: The double pole at the origin introduces $-180^\circ$ of phase lag at zero frequency. The infinite detour sweeps a full $360^\circ$ circle in the clockwise direction, guaranteeing that the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ is encircled twice clockwise ($N_{\text{cw}} = 2$). Since $P = 0$, $Z = 0 + 2 = 2$. A Type 2 third-order system with unity feedback is unconditionally unstable for all positive values of gain $K$ without phase lead compensation!

Archetype 4

Open-Loop Unstable System ($P = 1$): $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$ (JU CT 2024)

P = 1 • Requires $N_{\text{ccw}} = 1$
Open-Loop Unstable: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$ P = 1
Requires $N_{\text{ccw}} = 1$ Stable for $K > 1.0$

Crucial Takeaway: Unlike open-loop stable systems where stability requires $N = 0$, here $P = 1$, so stability demands $Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies N_{\text{ccw}} = 1$. The Nyquist plot must encircle $(-1 + j0)$ once in the counter-clockwise direction. This occurs if and only if the intercept $-K$ lies to the left of $-1$, which translates to $K > 1$!

Archetype 5

Conditionally Stable System (Dual Phase Crossovers)

Stable Only in Window $K_1 < K < K_2$
Conditionally Stable System: Two phase crossovers on negative real axis
Stable Window: $\frac{1}{a_2} < K < \frac{1}{a_1}$ (Reducing $K$ causes instability)

Unique Engineering Phenomenon: While standard intuition suggests that decreasing loop gain $K$ always increases stability, conditionally stable systems become unstable when gain is decreased below $K_1$! This often happens in systems with multiple zeros or lead-lag networks where phase lead is active only in an intermediate frequency band.

JU 2017 Q7

Stability Analysis via Routh and Nyquist Criterion

16 Marks
“Comment on the stability of the system whose open loop transfer function is given by: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{1}{s(2s+1)(s+1)}$$ Ascertain stability using both Routh-Hurwitz and Nyquist stability criteria.”

1 Method I: Algebraic Verification via Routh-Hurwitz Criterion

The closed-loop characteristic equation is:

$$1 + G(s)H(s) = 0 \implies s(2s+1)(s+1) + 1 = 0 \implies 2s^3 + 3s^2 + s + 1 = 0$$

Constructing the Routh array:

  • Row $s^3$: $2 \quad 1$
  • Row $s^2$: $3 \quad 1$
  • Row $s^1$: $\frac{3 \times 1 - 2 \times 1}{3} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.333$
  • Row $s^0$: $1$

First column elements: $\{+2, \; +3, \; +1/3, \; +1\}$. Zero sign changes occur. Therefore, all 3 closed-loop poles lie strictly in the LHP. The system is STABLE.

2 Method II: Frequency Domain Solution via Nyquist Stability Criterion

Step 1 (Open-Loop Poles): Poles are at $s = 0, -0.5, -1$. None lie in the open RHP $\implies P = 0$.

Step 2 (Frequency Response on $s = j\omega$):

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{1}{j\omega(1 + 2j\omega)(1 + j\omega)}$$ $$|G(j\omega)H(j\omega)| = \frac{1}{\omega \sqrt{1 + 4\omega^2}\sqrt{1 + \omega^2}}, \quad \angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(2\omega) - \tan^{-1}\omega$$

Step 3 (Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_{pc}$):

$$\angle G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ \implies \tan^{-1}(2\omega) + \tan^{-1}\omega = 90^\circ$$ $$\frac{2\omega + \omega}{1 - 2\omega^2} = \infty \implies 1 - 2\omega^2 = 0 \implies \omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.7071\text{ rad/s}$$

Step 4 (Magnitude at Phase Crossover):

$$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1 + 4(0.5)}\sqrt{1 + 0.5}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{3}\sqrt{1.5}} = \frac{1}{1.5} = \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.667$$

The Nyquist locus intersects the negative real axis at the point $\left( -\frac{2}{3}, \; 0 \right) = (-0.667, \; 0)$.

Step 5 (Encirclement Count & Stability Conclusion):

Because the real-axis crossing point lies at $-0.667 > -1$, the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ lies completely outside the closed Nyquist contour.

$$N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 - 0 = 0$$

Since $Z = 0$, there are zero closed-loop poles in the right-half $s$-plane. The system is strictly STABLE, fully corroborating Method I.

JU 2018 Q6(i)

Relationship between the $GH$-Plane and the $q(s)$-Plane

4.5 Marks
“Establish the relationship between the GH-plane and the q(s)-plane. The symbols carry their usual meanings.”

1. Analytical Formulation: Let $G(s)H(s) = u + jv$ be the open-loop transfer function. The characteristic function is defined as:

$$q(s) = 1 + G(s)H(s) = (1 + u) + jv = u_q + j v_q$$

Comparing real and imaginary coordinates:

$$u_q = u + 1 \iff u = u_q - 1, \qquad v_q = v$$

2. Conformal Translation Property: The transformation from the $GH$-plane to the $q(s)$-plane is a pure horizontal translation to the right by $+1$ unit along the real axis. The imaginary components are identically equal.

3. Equivalence of Singularities:

  • The origin $(0 + j0)$ of the $q(s)$-plane corresponds precisely to the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ of the $GH$-plane.
  • Encirclements of $(0, 0)$ by the locus of $q(s)$ equal encirclements of $(-1 + j0)$ by the locus of $G(s)H(s)$.
  • The zeros of $q(s)$ are the closed-loop poles; the poles of $q(s)$ are identical to the open-loop poles of $G(s)H(s)$.
JU 2018 Q8

Nyquist Plot and Stability Gain Boundary for $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(1+T_1 s)(1+T_2 s)}$

16 Marks
“For the system described by the following transfer function, sketch the Nyquist plot and ascertain the value of $K$ for which the closed loop system would be stable: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(1+T_1 s)(1+T_2 s)}$$”

1. Analytical Derivation of Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_{pc}$:

$$\angle G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc}) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_1) - \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_2) = -180^\circ$$ $$\tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_1) + \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_2) = 90^\circ \implies \frac{\omega_{pc}(T_1 + T_2)}{1 - \omega_{pc}^2 T_1 T_2} = \infty$$ $$1 - \omega_{pc}^2 T_1 T_2 = 0 \implies \omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}$$

2. Magnitude at Phase Crossover:

$$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{K}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}\sqrt{1 + \frac{T_1}{T_2}}\sqrt{1 + \frac{T_2}{T_1}}} = \frac{K}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}} \frac{T_1 + T_2}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}} = \frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2}$$

3. Stability Range: Open-loop poles are at $s = 0, -1/T_1, -1/T_2 \implies P = 0$. For closed-loop stability, $N = 0$, requiring the negative real crossing to be inside $-1$:

$$\frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2} < 1 \implies 0 < K < \frac{T_1 + T_2}{T_1 T_2}$$

The marginal gain is $K_{\text{mar}} = \frac{T_1 + T_2}{T_1 T_2}$, with sustained frequency of oscillation $\omega_{\text{osc}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}$ rad/s.

JU 2024 Supp Q3

Nyquist Criterion & Stability of Open-Loop Unstable System

16 Marks
“By use of Nyquist criterion, determine whether the closed-loop system having the following open-loop transfer function is stable or not. If not, how many closed-loop poles lie in the right half s-plane? $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s-1)}$$”
Figure 3.6: JU 2024 Supplementary Q3 Complete Closed Nyquist Plot
JU 2024 Supp Q3: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s-1)}$ P = 1
$N_{\text{ccw}} = 1$ $Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0$ Strictly Stable

Step 1: Open-Loop Singularities: Pole at $s = +1$ lies in the open RHP $\implies P = 1$. Zero at $s = -2$, pole at $s = -1$.

Step 2: Frequency Response: Along $s = j\omega$:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{j\omega + 2}{(j\omega + 1)(j\omega - 1)} = \frac{2 + j\omega}{-\omega^2 - 1} = \frac{-2}{1+\omega^2} - j \frac{\omega}{1+\omega^2}$$

At $\omega = 0$: $G(0)H(0) = -2 + j0$.

As $\omega \to \infty$: $\text{Re} \to 0, \; \text{Im} \to 0$. Terminal angle is $-90^\circ$.

Step 3: Encirclement Count: The locus starts at $(-2, 0)$, sweeps into the 3rd quadrant (Path $C_1$), reaches the origin, and reflects through the 2nd quadrant (Path $C_3$) back to $(-2, 0)$. This forms a closed oval in the LHP.

The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ lies strictly inside this loop. Tracing the direction $C_1 \to C_3$ shows that the critical point is encircled once in the counter-clockwise direction:

$$N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 1 = 0$$

Direct Algebraic Cross-Check: Closed-loop characteristic equation: $(s+1)(s-1) + (s+2) = s^2 - 1 + s + 2 = s^2 + s + 1 = 0$. Roots are $s = -0.5 \pm j0.866$, both in LHP. The system is strictly STABLE!

JU CT 2024 Q4

Nyquist Stability of Non-Minimum Phase Plant: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$

12 Marks
“The open loop transfer function of a unity feedback control system is $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$. Comment on the stability of the closed loop system using Nyquist Stability Criterion.”

1. Singularities & Open-Loop RHP Poles: Poles are at $s = 0, +1 \implies P = 1$.

2. Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_{pc}$:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K(3+j\omega)}{-\omega^2 - j\omega} = \frac{-K(3+j\omega)(\omega^2 - j\omega)}{\omega^4 + \omega^2} = \frac{-K(3\omega^2 + \omega^2) + j K(3\omega - \omega^3)}{\omega^4 + \omega^2}$$ $$\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = 0 \implies 3\omega - \omega^3 = 0 \implies \omega_{pc} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732\text{ rad/s}$$

3. Real-Axis Intercept:

$$\text{Re}[G(j\sqrt{3})H(j\sqrt{3})] = \frac{-4K(3)}{9 + 3} = \frac{-12K}{12} = -K$$

4. Complete Stability Conclusion: For closed-loop stability, $Z = 0 \implies N_{\text{ccw}} = P = 1$. The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ must be encircled once counter-clockwise. This occurs if and only if:

$$-K < -1 \implies K > 1$$

If $0 < K < 1$, $N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies Z = 1 - 0 = 1$ (Unstable, 1 RHP pole). If $K = 1$, marginally stable at $\omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{3}$ rad/s.

Master Formulas

Encirclement Criteria

Prof. MM Notes:
$N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z \iff Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}}$
Standard Texts (N&G):
$N_{\text{cw}} = Z - P \iff Z = P + N_{\text{cw}}$
Stability Condition: $Z = 0 \implies N_{\text{ccw}} = P$
Contour Detours ($C_4$)

Angular Sweep at Infinity

Type 0: No detour ($s=0$ ordinary).
Type 1 ($1/s$): $180^\circ$ CW arc at $\infty$ ($+90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to -90^\circ$).
Type 2 ($1/s^2$): $360^\circ$ full circle at $\infty$ ($+180^\circ \to 0^\circ \to -180^\circ$).
Asymptotes

Starting & Terminal Angles

Start ($\omega \to 0^+$): $\phi_0 = -N \times 90^\circ$
(Type 0: $0^\circ$, Type 1: $-90^\circ$, Type 2: $-180^\circ$)
End ($\omega \to \infty$): $\phi_\infty = -(n-m) \times 90^\circ$
(1st order: $-90^\circ$, 2nd: $-180^\circ$, 3rd: $-270^\circ$)
Type 1 Asymptote: $\sigma = -K \sum T_i$
Relative Stability

Gain & Phase Margins

Phase Crossover ($\omega_{pc}$):
$\angle GH(\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ \iff \text{Im}[GH] = 0$
Gain Margin:
$GM = 1 / |GH(\omega_{pc})|, \; GM_{\text{dB}} = -20\log_{10}a$
Phase Margin:
$PM = 180^\circ + \angle GH(\omega_{gc})$ where $|GH(\omega_{gc})| = 1$
Fatal Pitfalls

JU Examiner Traps to Avoid

• Open-Loop Unstable ($P > 0$): Stable does NOT mean $N = 0$! It requires $N_{\text{ccw}} = P$.
• Detour Direction: In $s$-plane detour is CW; mapped arc at $\infty$ is ALSO CW ($+90^\circ \to -90^\circ$).
• Mirror Image: Always remember Path $C_3$ is the conjugate reflection of $C_1$.
