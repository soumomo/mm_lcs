Open-Loop Unstable Plant • Indented Origin • Single RHP Pole

Stability Analysis of $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$ via Nyquist Stability Criterion

“The open loop transfer function of a unity feedback control system is $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$$ Comment on the stability of the closed loop system using Nyquist Stability Criterion. (CO4, K4)”
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularity Inventory & Stability Condition

Identify open-loop poles and zeros:

$$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+3)}{s(s-1)}$$
  • Open-loop Zero: $s = -3$ (lies in the Left-Half Plane, LHP).
  • Open-loop Poles: $s = 0$ (at origin), $s = +1$ (lies strictly in the open Right-Half Plane, RHP).

Consequently, the number of open-loop poles in the open Right-Half Plane is:

$$\mathbf{P = 1}$$

For the closed-loop system to be strictly stable, there must be zero closed-loop poles in the open RHP ($Z = 0$). According to the Nyquist Stability Criterion:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z = 1 - 0 = +1}$$

Fundamental Requirement: The complete closed Nyquist plot of $G(s)H(s)$ must encircle the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ exactly once in the counter-clockwise (CCW) direction for the closed-loop system to be stable.

Step 2: Frequency Response Formulation on Path $C_1$ ($s = j\omega, \omega: 0^+ \to +\infty$)

Substitute $s = j\omega$ into the open-loop transfer function:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K(j\omega + 3)}{j\omega(j\omega - 1)} = \frac{K(3 + j\omega)}{-\omega^2 - j\omega}$$

Multiplying numerator and denominator by $(-\omega^2 + j\omega)$ to separate real and imaginary parts:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K(3 + j\omega)(-\omega^2 + j\omega)}{\omega^4 + \omega^2} = \frac{K\left[-4\omega^2 + j\omega(3 - \omega^2)\right]}{\omega^2(\omega^2 + 1)}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{-4K}{\omega^2 + 1}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{K(3 - \omega^2)}{\omega(\omega^2 + 1)}}$$
Step 3: Phase Crossover Frequency & Real-Axis Intercept

The phase crossover frequency $\omega_{pc}$ occurs where the Nyquist locus crosses the real axis, i.e., where $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = 0$:

$$\frac{K(3 - \omega_{pc}^2)}{\omega_{pc}(\omega_{pc}^2 + 1)} = 0 \implies 3 - \omega_{pc}^2 = 0 \implies \mathbf{\omega_{pc} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.7320 \text{ rad/s}}$$

Evaluate the real part at this phase crossover frequency:

$$\text{Re}[G(j\sqrt{3})H(j\sqrt{3})] = \frac{-4K}{(\sqrt{3})^2 + 1} = \frac{-4K}{3 + 1} = \mathbf{-K}$$

Thus, the polar plot intersects the negative real axis at the coordinate $\mathbf{(-K, 0)}$.

Step 4: Mapping of All Segments of the Canonical Nyquist Contour
  • Path $C_1$ ($\omega: 0^+ \to +\infty$):
    As $\omega \to 0^+$: $\text{Re} \to -4K$ and $\text{Im} \to +\infty$. The locus starts at an asymptote at $\text{Re} = -4K$ in the 2nd quadrant.
    At $\omega = \sqrt{3}$: crosses the negative real axis at $(-K, 0)$ heading downward into the 3rd quadrant.
    As $\omega \to +\infty$: $|GH| \to 0, \angle GH \to -90^\circ$ (enters origin tangent to negative imaginary axis).
  • Path $C_2$ (Infinite Semicircle $s = R e^{j\theta}, R \to \infty, \theta: +90^\circ \to -90^\circ$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K s}{s^2} = \frac{K}{s} = \frac{K}{R} e^{-j\theta} \to 0$$ Maps to the origin $(0,0)$ in the $GH$-plane.
  • Path $C_3$ (Negative imaginary axis $\omega: -\infty \to 0^-$): Exact complex conjugate reflection of Path $C_1$ about the real axis. Leaves origin along $+90^\circ$, traverses the 2nd quadrant, crosses the real axis at $(-K, 0)$ at $\omega = -\sqrt{3}$ rad/s, and heads to $\text{Re} = -4K, \text{Im} \to -\infty$.
  • Path $C_4$ (Origin Indentation $s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}, \varepsilon \to 0, \theta: -90^\circ \to +90^\circ$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{3K}{\varepsilon e^{j\theta}(-1)} = \frac{3K}{\varepsilon} e^{-j(\theta + 180^\circ)}$$ As $\theta$ sweeps from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$, the angle sweeps from $-90^\circ \to -180^\circ \to -270^\circ \equiv +90^\circ$ (a $180^\circ$ clockwise arc of infinite radius in the left-half plane, connecting $-4K - j\infty$ to $-4K + j\infty$).
Step 5: Encirclement of Critical Point $(-1+j0)$ & Stability Conclusion

The closed Nyquist plot intersects the negative real axis at the point $(-K, 0)$. The critical point is $(-1 + j0)$.

  • Case 1 ($K > 1$): The intercept $-K$ lies to the left of $-1$ (i.e., $-K < -1$). The critical point $(-1+j0)$ is enclosed inside the loop formed by $C_1, C_3,$ and $C_4$. Tracing the contour demonstrates that $(-1+j0)$ is encircled once in the counter-clockwise (CCW) direction: $$N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 1 = 0 \quad \mathbf{(STABLE)}$$
  • Case 2 ($K = 1$): The Nyquist locus passes directly through the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ at frequency $\omega = \sqrt{3}$ rad/s. The closed-loop system is marginally stable, exhibiting sustained oscillations at $\omega = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732\text{ rad/s}$.
  • Case 3 ($0 < K < 1$): The intercept $-K$ lies to the right of $-1$ (i.e., $-K > -1$). The critical point $(-1+j0)$ lies outside the closed loop: $$N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 0 = 1 \quad \mathbf{(UNSTABLE, 1\text{ pole in RHP})}$$
Final Conclusion: The closed-loop system is strictly STABLE for $K > 1$, marginally stable at $K = 1$, and unstable for $0 < K < 1$.

Open-Loop Unstable Plant • Cauchy's Principle • Closed-Loop Stability

Stability Analysis of Open-Loop Unstable System: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s-1)}$

“By use of Nyquist criterion, determine whether the closed-loop system having the following open-loop transfer function is stable or not. If not, how many closed-loop poles lie in the right half $s$-plane? $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s-1)}$$”
Step 1: Open-Loop Poles in Right-Half Plane ($P$)

The open-loop transfer function is: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s-1)}$$

  • Open-loop Zero: $s = -2$ (LHP).
  • Open-loop Poles: Roots of $(s+1)(s-1) = 0 \implies s = -1$ (LHP) and $s = +1$ (RHP).

The pole at $s = +1$ lies in the open right-half $s$-plane ($\text{Re}(s) > 0$). Therefore:

$$\mathbf{P = 1}$$

Notice that there are no poles on the imaginary axis (no pole at origin), so no semicircular indentation is required.

Step 2: Frequency Response Mapping Along Path $C_1$ ($s = j\omega, \omega \in [0, +\infty)$)

Substitute $s = j\omega$ into $G(s)H(s)$:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{j\omega + 2}{(j\omega + 1)(j\omega - 1)} = \frac{2 + j\omega}{-\omega^2 - 1} = -\frac{2}{1 + \omega^2} - j \frac{\omega}{1 + \omega^2}$$

Key Geometric Deductions:

  • Real Part: $\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = -\frac{2}{1 + \omega^2} < 0$ for all $\omega \ge 0$.
  • Imaginary Part: $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = -\frac{\omega}{1 + \omega^2} \le 0$ for all $\omega \ge 0$.
  • Thus, for all $\omega \in [0, \infty)$, the polar plot lies strictly and entirely in the third quadrant!
  • At $\omega = 0$: $G(j0)H(j0) = -2 - j0 = \mathbf{-2}$.
  • As $\omega \to +\infty$: $\lim_{\omega \to \infty} G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = 0^- - j0$ (enters origin along $-180^\circ$).
  • Peak Negative Imaginary Part: Set $\frac{d}{d\omega}\left[\frac{\omega}{1+\omega^2}\right] = 0 \implies 1 - \omega^2 = 0 \implies \omega = 1\text{ rad/s}$.
    At $\omega = 1$: $G(j1)H(j1) = -\frac{2}{2} - j\frac{1}{2} = \mathbf{-1 - j0.5}$.
Step 3: Mapping of Remaining Contour Segments
  • Path $C_2$ (Infinite Semicircle $s = R e^{j\theta}, R \to \infty, \theta: +90^\circ \to -90^\circ$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{s}{s^2} = \frac{1}{s} = \frac{1}{R} e^{-j\theta} \to 0$$ Maps to the origin $(0,0)$.
  • Path $C_3$ (Negative Imaginary Axis $s = -j\omega, \omega: -\infty \to 0^-$): Complex conjugate reflection across the real axis: lies entirely in the second quadrant ($\text{Re} < 0, \text{Im} \ge 0$), passing through $-1 + j0.5$ at $\omega = -1$ rad/s and returning to $-2 + j0$ as $\omega \to 0^-$.
Step 4: Encirclement Analysis & Closed-Loop Stability

The complete closed Nyquist plot intersects the negative real axis at only one point: $\mathbf{-2 + j0}$ (at $\omega = 0$).

The critical point is $\mathbf{-1 + j0}$. Since $-2$ lies to the left of $-1$ ($-2 < -1$), the closed Nyquist locus encloses the critical point. Tracing the path from $\omega = 0 \to +\infty$ (3rd quadrant) and $\omega = -\infty \to 0$ (2nd quadrant) shows that $(-1 + j0)$ is encircled in the counter-clockwise (CCW) direction:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = +1}$$

Applying the Nyquist Stability Criterion:

$$Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 1 = \mathbf{0}$$
Conclusion:
  1. The number of closed-loop poles in the right-half $s$-plane is $\mathbf{Z = 0}$.
  2. Therefore, the closed-loop system is strictly STABLE!

Negative Plant Gain • RHP Pole • Origin Indentation

Stability Analysis of $G(s)H(s) = \frac{-1}{2s(1 - 20s)}$ via Nyquist Plot

“Sketch the Nyquist plot for a system with the following open-loop transfer function. Determine the stability of the system. (CO4) $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{-1}{2s(1 - 20s)}$$”
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularities & RHP Pole Count ($P$)

The open-loop transfer function is:

$$G(s)H(s) = \frac{-1}{2s(1 - 20s)} = \frac{1}{2s(20s - 1)}$$
  • Open-loop pole at origin: $s = 0$.
  • Open-loop pole from $(1 - 20s) = 0 \implies s = +\frac{1}{20} = +0.05$ (lies strictly in the open Right-Half Plane).

Therefore, the number of open-loop RHP poles is:

$$\mathbf{P = 1}$$

For closed-loop stability ($Z = 0$), the Nyquist plot must satisfy:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z = 1 - 0 = +1}$$
Step 2: Frequency Response Formulation Along Path $C_1$ ($s = j\omega, \omega: 0^+ \to +\infty$)

Substitute $s = j\omega$:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{-1}{2j\omega(1 - j20\omega)} = \frac{-1}{j2\omega + 40\omega^2} = \frac{-1}{40\omega^2 + j2\omega}$$

Rationalizing the denominator:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{-(40\omega^2 - j2\omega)}{(40\omega^2)^2 + (2\omega)^2} = \frac{-40\omega^2 + j2\omega}{1600\omega^4 + 4\omega^2} = \frac{-40\omega^2 + j2\omega}{4\omega^2(400\omega^2 + 1)}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{-10}{400\omega^2 + 1}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{1}{2\omega(400\omega^2 + 1)}}$$

Key Deductions for $\omega > 0$:

  • $\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] < 0$ for all $\omega > 0$.
  • $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] > 0$ for all $\omega > 0$.
  • The polar curve lies strictly and entirely in the second quadrant!
  • As $\omega \to 0^+$: $\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \text{Re} = -10$ and $\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \text{Im} = +\infty$. Thus, the locus approaches a vertical asymptote at $\mathbf{\text{Re} = -10}$.
  • As $\omega \to +\infty$: $\text{Re} \to 0^-$ and $\text{Im} \to 0^+$. The locus enters the origin tangent to the positive imaginary axis ($+90^\circ$).
Step 3: Indentation Around Origin on Path $C_4$ ($s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}, \theta: -90^\circ \to +90^\circ$)

Near $s = 0$, with $\varepsilon \to 0$:

$$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{-1}{2(\varepsilon e^{j\theta})(1 - 0)} = \frac{-1}{2\varepsilon e^{j\theta}} = \frac{1}{2\varepsilon} e^{j(180^\circ - \theta)}$$

As $\theta$ sweeps from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$:

$$\text{Angle sweeps: } 180^\circ - (-90^\circ) = +270^\circ \longrightarrow 180^\circ \longrightarrow +90^\circ$$

This describes a circular arc of infinite radius ($R_\infty = \frac{1}{2\varepsilon} \to \infty$) sweeping clockwise by $180^\circ$ through the left-half plane, connecting $-10 - j\infty$ (end of $C_3$) to $-10 + j\infty$ (start of $C_1$) via $(-\infty, 0)$.

Step 4: Encirclement Analysis & Closed-Loop Stability

The closed contour is bounded by the vertical asymptote at $\text{Re} = -10$ and the infinite left-half plane closure arc.

The critical point is $\mathbf{-1 + j0}$. Since $\text{Re} = -1 > -10$, the critical point lies to the right of the vertical asymptote and inside the closed loop formed by $C_1$, $C_3$, and $C_4$. Tracing the loop reveals that the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ is encircled once in the counter-clockwise (CCW) direction:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = +1}$$

Applying the Nyquist Stability Criterion:

$$Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 1 = \mathbf{0}$$
Conclusion: Because $Z = 0$, all closed-loop poles lie in the left-half $s$-plane. The closed-loop system is strictly STABLE.

Type 2 Systems • Double Pole at Origin • 360° Enclosure

Nyquist Analysis for Type 2 Systems with Double Pole at Origin

Part A • JU 2024 Regular Q3 (Option 1) [12 Marks]:
“By use of Nyquist criterion, determine whether the closed-loop system having the following open-loop transfer function is stable or not. If, not how many closed-loop poles lie in the right half s-plane? (CO4) $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{1+4s}{s^2(1+s)(1+2s)}$$”
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularities & RHP Pole Count ($P$)

Open-loop poles are roots of $s^2(1+s)(1+2s) = 0$:

$$s_1 = 0, \quad s_2 = 0, \quad s_3 = -1, \quad s_4 = -0.5$$

No open-loop poles lie in the open right-half $s$-plane ($\text{Re}(s) > 0$):

$$\mathbf{P = 0}$$
Step 2: Mapping of Indented Origin Semicircle ($s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}, \theta: -90^\circ \to +90^\circ$)

Near $s = 0$, with $\varepsilon \to 0$:

$$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{1}{(\varepsilon e^{j\theta})^2 (1)(1)} = \frac{1}{\varepsilon^2} e^{-j2\theta}$$

As $\theta$ sweeps from $-90^\circ \to 0^\circ \to +90^\circ$ (CCW in $s$-plane):

$$\text{Angle in } GH\text{-plane sweeps: } +180^\circ \longrightarrow 0^\circ \longrightarrow -180^\circ$$

This describes an arc of infinite radius $R_\infty = \frac{1}{\varepsilon^2} \to \infty$ sweeping clockwise by $360^\circ$ (a full circle) through the right-half plane.

Step 3: Frequency Response Formulation on Path $C_1$ ($s = j\omega, \omega: 0^+ \to +\infty$)

Substitute $s = j\omega$:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{1 + j4\omega}{-\omega^2(1 + j\omega)(1 + j2\omega)} = \frac{1 + j4\omega}{-\omega^2[(1 - 2\omega^2) + j3\omega]}$$

Multiplying numerator and denominator by $[(1 - 2\omega^2) - j3\omega]$:

$$(1 + j4\omega)[(1 - 2\omega^2) - j3\omega] = (1 - 2\omega^2 + 12\omega^2) + j[4\omega(1 - 2\omega^2) - 3\omega] = (1 + 10\omega^2) + j\omega(1 - 8\omega^2)$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Re}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = -\frac{1 + 10\omega^2}{\omega^2[(1 - 2\omega^2)^2 + 9\omega^2]}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = -\frac{\omega(1 - 8\omega^2)}{\omega^2[(1 - 2\omega^2)^2 + 9\omega^2]} = \frac{8\omega^2 - 1}{\omega[(1 - 2\omega^2)^2 + 9\omega^2]}}$$
Step 4: Phase Crossover Frequency & Real-Axis Intercept

Set $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = 0$:

$$1 - 8\omega_{pc}^2 = 0 \implies \omega_{pc}^2 = \frac{1}{8} \implies \mathbf{\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{8}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.3536\text{ rad/s}}$$

Substitute $\omega_{pc}^2 = \frac{1}{8} = 0.125$ into the real part:

$$\text{Re}[G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})] = -\frac{1 + 10(0.125)}{0.125\left[(1 - 2(0.125))^2 + 9(0.125)\right]} = -\frac{2.25}{0.125[0.5625 + 1.125]} = -\frac{2.25}{0.125 \times 1.6875} = \mathbf{-10.667}$$

The Nyquist plot intersects the negative real axis at $\mathbf{-10.667}$.

Step 5: Encirclement & Stability Conclusion

The negative real axis is crossed at $-10.667$. The critical point is $\mathbf{-1 + j0}$.

Because $-10.667 < -1$, the critical point lies to the right of the real axis intercept. When combining Path $C_1$, the infinite $360^\circ$ clockwise circle of Path $C_4$, and conjugate Path $C_3$, the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ is enclosed by the loop and encircled twice in the clockwise (CW) direction:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{cw}} = 2 \iff N_{\text{ccw}} = -2}$$

Applying the Nyquist Stability Criterion:

$$\mathbf{Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 - (-2) = 2}$$
Conclusion: The closed-loop system is strictly UNSTABLE, and exactly $\mathbf{Z = 2}$ closed-loop poles lie in the right-half $s$-plane.
Part B • JU 2019 Regular Q7 (Option OR) [10 Marks]:
“For the system described by the following open loop transfer function, sketch the Nyquist plot and ascertain the stability of the closed loop system. $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s^2(1+s)(1+2s)}$$”
Solution for $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s^2(1+s)(1+2s)}$
  • $P = 0$ (open-loop poles at $s = 0, 0, -1, -0.5$).
  • Along $s = j\omega$: $$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{-K}{\omega^2(1+j\omega)(1+j2\omega)} = \frac{-K(1-2\omega^2) + j3K\omega}{\omega^2[(1-2\omega^2)^2 + 9\omega^2]}$$ Notice that $\text{Im}[G(j\omega)H(j\omega)] = \frac{3K}{\omega[(1-2\omega^2)^2 + 9\omega^2]} > 0$ for all $\omega > 0$. The entire polar locus lies strictly in the Upper Half-Plane!
  • The origin indentation $s = \varepsilon e^{j\theta}$ maps to a massive $360^\circ$ clockwise circle of infinite radius ($K/\varepsilon^2$).
  • The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ is encircled twice in the clockwise direction ($N_{\text{cw}} = 2$).
  • $$Z = P + N_{\text{cw}} = 0 + 2 = \mathbf{2}$$
Conclusion: The system is unconditionally UNSTABLE for all $K > 0$, with 2 closed-loop poles in the RHP.

Exact Circular Nyquist Locus • Open-Loop Unstable Plant

Stability Range of $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+1)}{1-s}$ via Nyquist Criterion

“For the system described by the following open loop transfer function, sketch the Nyquist plot. Ascertain the range of gain $K$ for which the closed loop system will remain stable using Nyquist Stability criterion only. (CO4) $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+1)}{1-s} \quad (K > 0)$$”
Step 1: Open-Loop Singularity Inventory & Stability Requirement

Identify open-loop poles and zeros:

$$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K(s+1)}{1-s}$$
  • Zero: $s = -1$ (LHP).
  • Pole: $s = +1$ (lies in open Right-Half Plane).

Number of open-loop RHP poles is:

$$\mathbf{P = 1}$$

For closed-loop stability ($Z = 0$), the complete Nyquist plot must encircle the critical point $(-1+j0)$ once in the counter-clockwise direction:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = P - Z = 1 - 0 = +1}$$
Step 2: Frequency Response Mapping Along $s = j\omega$ ($\omega: 0 \to +\infty$)

Substitute $s = j\omega$:

$$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K(1+j\omega)}{1-j\omega} = K \frac{(1+j\omega)^2}{1+\omega^2} = K \left[\frac{1-\omega^2}{1+\omega^2} + j \frac{2\omega}{1+\omega^2}\right]$$

Magnitude and Phase:

$$|G(j\omega)H(j\omega)| = K \frac{\sqrt{1+\omega^2}}{\sqrt{1+\omega^2}} = \mathbf{K \quad \text{(CONSTANT for all $\omega$!)}}$$ $$\angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \tan^{-1}\omega - (-\tan^{-1}\omega) = \mathbf{2\tan^{-1}\omega}$$
  • At $\omega = 0$: $G(j0)H(j0) = +K \angle 0^\circ$ (on positive real axis).
  • At $\omega = 1$: $G(j1)H(j1) = K \angle +90^\circ = +jK$ (on positive imaginary axis).
  • As $\omega \to +\infty$: $\lim_{\omega \to \infty} G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = K \angle +180^\circ = -K$ (on negative real axis).
  • Path $C_1$ describes an exact semicircle of radius $K$ in the upper half-plane traversed counter-clockwise from $+K$ to $-K$!
Step 3: Mapping of Semicircle at Infinity and Negative Imaginary Axis
  • Path $C_2$ ($s = R e^{j\theta}, R \to \infty$): $$G(s)H(s) \approx \frac{K s}{-s} = -K$$ Maps to the single point $-K$ on the negative real axis.
  • Path $C_3$ ($s = -j\omega, \omega: -\infty \to 0$): Complex conjugate reflection describes the lower half semicircle of radius $K$ from $-K$ back to $+K$ counter-clockwise!

The complete closed Nyquist plot is an exact circle centered at the origin with radius $K$, traversed in the counter-clockwise (CCW) direction!

Step 4: Encirclement of Critical Point $(-1+j0)$ & Range of $K$

The circle of radius $K$ intersects the negative real axis at the point $\mathbf{-K}$.

  • If $K > 1$: The radius of the circle is greater than 1. The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ lies strictly inside the circle. Because the circle is traversed counter-clockwise: $$N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 1 = 0 \quad \mathbf{(STABLE)}$$
  • If $K = 1$: The circle passes directly through $(-1+j0)$. The system is marginally stable.
  • If $0 < K < 1$: The critical point $(-1 + j0)$ lies outside the circle: $$N_{\text{ccw}} = 0 \implies Z = P - N_{\text{ccw}} = 1 - 0 = 1 \quad \mathbf{(UNSTABLE, 1\text{ pole in RHP})}$$
Final Answer: The closed-loop system will remain stable if and only if: $$\mathbf{K > 1}$$

Type 1 Third-Order Canonical Benchmark • Exact Margin Evaluation

Complete Nyquist Plot for Type 1 Plant: $G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(1+T_1 s)(1+T_2 s)}$

Part A • JU 2018 Regular Q8 [16 Marks]:
“For the system described by the following transfer function, sketch the Nyquist plot and ascertain the value of $K$ for which the closed loop system would be stable: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{K}{s(1+T_1 s)(1+T_2 s)}$$”
Step 1: Open-Loop Poles & Stability Condition

Poles are at $s = 0, -1/T_1, -1/T_2$. None lie in the open right-half plane:

$$\mathbf{P = 0}$$

For closed-loop stability ($Z = 0$), the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ must not be encircled:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{ccw}} = 0}$$
Step 2: Frequency Response Along $s = j\omega$ ($\omega: 0^+ \to \infty$) $$G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = \frac{K}{j\omega(1 + j\omega T_1)(1 + j\omega T_2)}$$ $$|G(j\omega)H(j\omega)| = \frac{K}{\omega \sqrt{1 + \omega^2 T_1^2} \sqrt{1 + \omega^2 T_2^2}}$$ $$\angle G(j\omega)H(j\omega) = -90^\circ - \tan^{-1}(\omega T_1) - \tan^{-1}(\omega T_2)$$

Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_{pc}$: Set $\angle GH = -180^\circ$:

$$\tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_1) + \tan^{-1}(\omega_{pc} T_2) = 90^\circ \implies \frac{\omega_{pc}(T_1 + T_2)}{1 - \omega_{pc}^2 T_1 T_2} = \infty \implies 1 - \omega_{pc}^2 T_1 T_2 = 0$$ $$\mathbf{\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}}$$
Step 3: Negative Real-Axis Intercept & Stability Condition on $K$

Evaluate magnitude at $\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}}$:

$$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{K}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{T_1}{T_2}} \sqrt{1 + \frac{T_2}{T_1}}} = \frac{K \sqrt{T_1 T_2}}{\sqrt{\frac{(T_1 + T_2)^2}{T_1 T_2}}} = \mathbf{\frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2}}$$

The Nyquist plot crosses the negative real axis at $-\frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2}$. For the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ not to be encircled:

$$-\frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2} > -1 \iff \mathbf{K < \frac{T_1 + T_2}{T_1 T_2}}$$
Part B • JU 2017 Regular Q7 [16 Marks]:
“Comment on the stability of the system whose open loop transfer function is given by: $$G(s)H(s) = \frac{1}{s(2s + 1)(s + 1)}$$”
Exact Solution for $G(s)H(s) = \frac{1}{s(2s+1)(s+1)}$

Here, $K = 1$, $T_1 = 2\text{ s}$, and $T_2 = 1\text{ s}$. Open-loop RHP poles $P = 0$.

  1. Phase Crossover Frequency: $$\omega_{pc} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T_1 T_2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \times 1}} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.7071\text{ rad/s}}$$
  2. Negative Real-Axis Intercept: $$|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})| = \frac{K T_1 T_2}{T_1 + T_2} = \frac{1 \times 2 \times 1}{2 + 1} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3} \approx 0.6667}$$ The polar plot crosses the negative real axis at $\mathbf{-0.6667}$.
  3. Encirclement of $(-1+j0)$: Since $-0.6667 > -1$, the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ lies outside the closed Nyquist contour: $$N = 0 \implies Z = P - N = 0 - 0 = \mathbf{0}$$
  4. Gain Margin: $$GM = \frac{1}{a} = \frac{1}{2/3} = \mathbf{1.5} \implies GM_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(1.5) = \mathbf{+3.52\text{ dB}}$$
Conclusion: Because $Z = 0$ and $GM = +3.52\text{ dB} > 0$, the closed-loop system is strictly STABLE.

Archival Theory & Short Notes on Polar Plots and Stability Mapping

Part A • JU 2024 Supplementary Q5(iii) [8 Marks]:
“Write short notes: Ascertaining phase margin and gain margin from open loop polar plot.”
1. Concept of Relative Stability on Open-Loop Polar Plot

The open-loop polar plot is a plot of the magnitude and phase of $G(j\omega)H(j\omega)$ in the complex plane as frequency $\omega$ varies from $0$ to $+\infty$. Gain Margin ($GM$) and Phase Margin ($PM$) measure the closeness of the polar locus to the critical point $(-1 + j0)$, quantifying the safety buffer against instability.

2. Ascertaining Gain Margin ($GM$)
  • Phase Crossover Frequency ($\omega_{pc}$): The frequency where the open-loop phase angle equals $-180^\circ$. Graphically, this is the frequency where the polar plot intersects the negative real axis: $$\angle G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc}) = -180^\circ$$
  • Measurement ($a$): Measure the distance $a = |G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|$ from the origin to the intersection point on the negative real axis.
  • Calculation: Gain Margin is defined as the reciprocal of this intercept: $$\mathbf{GM = \frac{1}{a} = \frac{1}{|G(j\omega_{pc})H(j\omega_{pc})|}}$$ $$\mathbf{GM_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{a}\right) = -20 \log_{10} a}$$
3. Ascertaining Phase Margin ($PM$)
  • Gain Crossover Frequency ($\omega_{gc}$): The frequency where the magnitude of $G(j\omega)H(j\omega)$ equals unity ($1.0$). Graphically, this is the intersection of the polar plot with the unit circle centered at the origin: $$|G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})| = 1.0$$
  • Calculation: Draw a radial line from the origin to this intersection point on the unit circle. The phase angle is $\phi_{gc} = \angle G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})$. The Phase Margin is: $$\mathbf{PM = 180^\circ + \phi_{gc} = 180^\circ + \angle G(j\omega_{gc})H(j\omega_{gc})}$$
Geometric representation of Gain Margin ($GM = 1/a$) and Phase Margin ($PM = 180^\circ + \phi_{gc}$) on the Nyquist Polar Plot.
4. Relative Stability Interpretation
  • Stable: $\omega_{gc} < \omega_{pc} \iff a < 1 \iff GM > 1\text{ }(GM_{\text{dB}} > 0\text{ dB})$ and $PM > 0^\circ$.
  • Marginally Stable: $\omega_{gc} = \omega_{pc} \iff a = 1 \iff GM = 1\text{ }(0\text{ dB})$ and $PM = 0^\circ$.
  • Unstable: $\omega_{gc} > \omega_{pc} \iff a > 1 \iff GM < 1\text{ }(GM_{\text{dB}} < 0\text{ dB})$ and $PM < 0^\circ$.
Part B • JU 2018 Regular Q6(i) [4.5 Marks]:
“Establish the relationship between GH-plane and q(s) plane. The symbols carry their usual meanings.”
1. Fundamental Definitions

Let $G(s)H(s)$ be the open-loop transfer function of a closed-loop feedback control system. The closed-loop characteristic equation is given by:

$$q(s) = 1 + G(s)H(s) = 0$$
  • $GH$-Plane: The complex plane where the axes represent the real ($u$) and imaginary ($v$) parts of the open-loop frequency response: $$G(s)H(s) = u + jv$$
  • $q(s)$-Plane: The complex plane where the axes represent the real ($u_q$) and imaginary ($v_q$) parts of the characteristic function: $$q(s) = u_q + jv_q$$
2. Analytical Relationship

Substitute $G(s)H(s) = u + jv$ into $q(s)$:

$$q(s) = 1 + (u + jv) = (1 + u) + jv$$

Equating real and imaginary parts:

$$\mathbf{u_q = u + 1}$$ $$\mathbf{v_q = v}$$

Geometric Deduction: The locus of $q(s)$ in the $q(s)$-plane is identical in shape, scale, and orientation to the locus of $G(s)H(s)$ in the $GH$-plane, translated horizontally to the right by $\mathbf{+1}$ unit along the real axis.

3. Equivalence of Encirclements & Nyquist Criterion

According to Cauchy's Principle of Argument, the number of clockwise encirclements of the origin $(0,0)$ by the locus of $q(s)$ in the $q(s)$-plane equals $N = P - Z$.

Because $q(s) = 0 \iff G(s)H(s) = -1 + j0$, setting $u_q = 0, v_q = 0$ gives:

$$u = u_q - 1 = 0 - 1 = -1, \quad v = v_q = 0$$

Therefore, the origin $(0 + j0)$ of the $q(s)$-plane corresponds identically to the critical point $(-1 + j0)$ in the $GH$-plane:

$$\mathbf{N_{\text{origin in } q(s)\text{-plane}} \equiv N_{(-1+j0)\text{ in } GH\text{-plane}}}$$
Practical Importance: Plotting in the $GH$-plane avoids having to add $+1$ point-by-point to $G(s)H(s)$. Moreover, the effect of varying open-loop gain $K$ can be evaluated directly on the same plot by checking the position of $-1/K$ relative to the locus, without re-plotting the entire function.
